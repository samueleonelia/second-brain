# Market the Problem More Than Anything Else

## The Story

Most creators think about content in terms of top-of-funnel versus bottom-of-funnel. Erica Schneider threw that model out for social. On a social feed you cannot control who sees a post or when, so she argues the funnel framing is silly there, and she replaced it with a four-tier hierarchy sorted by how often each tier should appear.

The tier she posts most is what she calls marketing the problem. The instruction is specific: identify a problem you can articulate better than anyone else in the world, make it highly visible, and present yourself as the solution to it. This is not selling; it is repeatedly naming the pain your reader feels so that you become associated with it. She did exactly this with editing, saying "the way that I rose up was talking about editing," making the case that everyone was thinking about editing wrong and offering her framing.

Beneath that sits build-in-public content: launch updates, milestones, the emotional and relatable posts like realizing she had been a solopreneur for a year. Those posts fly because they are aspirational, but she keeps them a step below problem content. Third is series content, the recurring posts tied to a newsletter drop or a podcast episode. Last, and least frequent, is overwhelming proof, the results content that answers "can you actually get the results that you say you can?" She stresses this is not a testimonial saying it was great to work with her but a concrete business outcome a client got, and it should run less often than everything else.

The ordering is the mechanism. By weighting the mix heavily toward the problem and only lightly toward proof and promotion, the audience is trained on what she solves long before she asks for anything, which is what makes the occasional proof or launch post land instead of reading as a pitch.

## Lesson for Creators

Do not spread your content evenly across "value" and "sales." Rank it. Spend the majority of your posts making one specific problem vivid and positioning yourself as its answer, a smaller share building in public and running a series, and the smallest share on hard proof. A reader who has seen you name their problem twenty times reads your one proof post as evidence, not advertising.
