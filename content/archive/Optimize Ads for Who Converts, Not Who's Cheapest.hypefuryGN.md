# Optimize Ads for Who Converts, Not Who's Cheapest

## The Story

By the time Geoff Sharpe described his ad engine on the Creator Spotlight podcast (2025), roughly 90% of Lookout Media's growth had come from Meta, at a cost of about 60 to 70 cents per lead on the cheap end — the kind of very low local acquisition cost that makes a lot of operators chase the lowest number they can find. Geoff argues that number is a trap if you read it in isolation. Lookout's list had grown to around 100,000 free subscribers, but only about 3,000 of those were paying members, and the whole business runs on that conversion, not on raw list size.

So he optimizes for the far end of the funnel. Ads that promise food content pull cheap leads, but those people convert to paying members at a much lower rate than leads that came in on a news hook. _"while I pay like more now for leads, the quality of the lead is much higher,"_ he said, because _"the end goal that I'm optimizing for is very different"_ than simply buying the cheapest signup. He is explicit that a low CPA can be a mirage: _"there is a difference between acquiring someone who like may open a newsletter for a little bit versus acquiring someone who's eventually going to give you"_ a hundred dollars a year.

The discipline behind this is lifetime value. Because Lookout decided from the start to be a membership business, the only acquisition question that matters is what a subscriber is eventually worth as a member. That single north-star metric — LTV, not CPA — is what justifies buying Meta ads at all, and it flips which "expensive" leads are actually the bargain.

## Lesson for Creators

Cheap subscribers are only cheap if they do the thing you need them to do. Decide the downstream action you are really buying — a paid membership, a purchase, a renewal — and optimize spend against that conversion, even when it means paying more per lead. A higher cost-per-acquisition on a lead that converts beats a rock-bottom lead that opens twice and churns. Know your lifetime value first; it tells you which leads are worth overpaying for.
