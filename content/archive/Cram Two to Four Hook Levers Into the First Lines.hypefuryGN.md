# Cram Two to Four Hook Levers Into the First Lines

## The Story

Tommy Clark treats the LinkedIn hook, the first one to three sentences before the *see more* button, as the highest-leverage part of any post: get it wrong and the rest is buried. His framework is to think of a fixed menu of *hook levers* and to deliberately pack several into those opening lines rather than leaning on just one. As he describes it, "I'm always trying to include anywhere from two to four hook levers in each hook."

The levers are named and reusable. Negativity bias. Specific numbers and metrics. Trendjacking, which piggybacks on a live news story (he cites how the Barbie movie's marketing wave gave everyone on LinkedIn a shared thing to post about). Credibility-jacking, the evergreen cousin, which borrows a known name or company: leading with a famous operator's name earns a stranger's attention that *here's my strategy* would not. And the simple list, *here are five lessons*, which opens a loop that pulls the reader into the post.

The failure mode he sees is the one-lever hook, a bare list or a lone narrative opener. His instruction to his own team is to raise the count: "you want to use at least two and really up to four or five hook levers." The only ceiling is legibility. Stack as many as you can without the opening becoming a jumbled pile-up. The more levers packed cleanly into those first lines, the more of the audience clears the "see more" threshold and reads on.

## Lesson for Creators

Stop writing hooks by feel. Keep a checklist of hook levers, negativity bias, a specific number, a trend to jack, a famous name to borrow, a list that opens a loop, a story, and consciously build two to four of them into your first three lines. When you post your first thing tomorrow, write three different hook variations and pick the one carrying the most levers before you commit. The body can be excellent, but the hook is what decides whether anyone reaches it.
