# Megaphone Productizes Cross-Creator Amplification

## The Story

Houck had already learned that peer engagement was the real distribution engine for a creator. Megaphone is that lesson turned into software. He described it as a subscription SaaS that lets you "route your content to big creators for amplification," where the creators in the network set their own rates and you fund the payouts directly. The manual favor-trading of DMs and group chats becomes a marketplace anyone can buy into.

The early proof points were concrete. Houck cited "a thread hitting 1.6M impressions" and a LinkedIn page growing from zero to 2,500 followers in 24 hours through the network's coordinated engagement. The mechanism is timing as much as reach: when several established accounts engage with a post early, the platform's algorithm reads it as worth showing to more people.

He had earned the right to build it. After growing his own audience past 100,000, Houck concluded that "success with audience building comes down to a combination of quality content and timely, relevant engagement" — and Megaphone sells exactly the second half, the engagement, to people who already have the first.

He treated the product as a step ladder, not a moonshot: skateboard, then bike, then motorcycle, then car. Early customers were founders and ghostwriting agencies, and he planned to expand to new niches only after seeing a repeatable growth system, funding it from revenue rather than raising.

## Lesson for Creators

The tactic you do by hand is often a product someone will pay for. Houck spent years trading engagement with peers, then packaged that exact behavior into a marketplace where amplification has a price. If a growth move works repeatedly and depends on a network you have built, productizing it turns your relationships into a revenue stream instead of a series of one-off favors.
