# Make the Writer the Thing Readers Trust

## The Story

Lookout Media's food coverage barely converts anyone to a paid subscription — Geoff Sharpe tested a paywall on it and readers would not pay. On paper it looks like a losing column. But it earns its place by building something the news content can't, and the load-bearing part is the writer as a person, not the brand. On the Creator Spotlight podcast (2025) Geoff put it plainly: _"it's not just our brand that people trust, it's the food writer themselves."_

So the writer is made deliberately visible. _"we have a picture of them in the newsletter so people know who they are,"_ he said — the critic is not anonymous, and they are sent to affordable mom-and-pop spots, not just expensive rooms, so readers come to rely on a named human's taste. Geoff is emphatic that this trust is fragile and non-negotiable: Lookout never takes payment for a review, because _"trust is so hard to build and so easy to lose."_ The moment a review can be bought, the asset evaporates.

The payoff shows up in adjacent revenue. That personal trust let Lookout run its first live event with _"$120 tickets sold out in a week."_ Readers were not buying the writing; they were buying access to a person they had come to trust through the writing. Email is the channel that makes this work, because it builds a relationship not just with the publication but with the individual who lands in the inbox — the same dynamic Geoff sees in operators like Ryan Sneddon of Naptown Scoop, whose face became his marketing.

## Lesson for Creators

Some content will never convert directly, yet still be worth producing because of the trust it builds in a named person. Put a real face and byline on the work, hold that trust as sacred (no pay-for-praise, ever), and then monetize the relationship rather than the article — through events, memberships, or access. People pay to be near someone they trust more readily than they pay for the content that earned the trust.
