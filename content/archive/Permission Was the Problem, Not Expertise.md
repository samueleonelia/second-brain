# Permission Was the Problem, Not Expertise

## The Story

When she finally crossed 22,000 followers in six months, Mischa Collins itemized what had blocked her for the years before that:

"I convinced myself I had nothing to say. I thought nobody wanted to hear my story. I wasn't an expert, so who would listen?"

Her conclusion: "But, you don't need to be an expert. You just need to start, have a willingness to learn and to keep going."

She returned to the same theme in a later post about choosing to stop being invisible: "Waiting to feel 'ready' to post. Talking yourself out of your ability. Letting competitors steal your opportunities. Staying invisible because you feel annoying. Hiding behind perfection instead of executing."

Her replacement script: "Show up before you feel ready. Get clear on what you stand for. Build trust long before you need to sell. Don't be afraid to celebrate your wins publicly. Share what you know even if you're not an expert."

The final formulation, written as a comment on her own retrospective post: "You don't need a plan → just action. You don't need results → just willingness to learn. You don't need followers → just belief. That's the truth about personal branding: No one hands it to you. No one tells you what to do. No one backs you at the start (other than you)."

## Lesson for Creators

"I'm not an expert yet" is the cleanest excuse in the creator playbook because it sounds like humility. It isn't. Most audiences don't want experts; they want someone slightly ahead of them, narrating the journey as it happens. The permission-seeking pose is what keeps creators waiting, and the wait is what costs them years of compounding. The thing that finally made Mischa post wasn't gaining expertise; it was deciding she didn't need it.
