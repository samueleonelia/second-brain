# A Plan Document Can't Hold Your Product Vision

Ras Mic's core argument against fire-and-forget agent loops is an analogy: hire a very smart developer, hand them a spec, and let them build the entire product without consulting you. They will fill every gap with assumptions about look, feel, and architecture, and many will diverge from your vision. "You might think your plan document covers everything. But truth to the matter, it never does. There's always an edge case. There's always something that's missed."

An autonomous loop has the same failure mode, because a spec.md or prd.md is asked to carry judgment that hasn't been formed yet. Anyone who has run a service business knows clients can never fully externalize what they want even to another human; an agent reading a document gets less, not more. The exception is work where the details don't matter: Mic built an Among Us simulator to benchmark which AI models lie best in about ninety minutes with a single fire-and-forget instruction, and it got many details wrong that he never cared about. For meaningful products his conclusion is blunt: "Human in the loop is the best loop," because "AI can replicate sauce. It can't create sauce."
