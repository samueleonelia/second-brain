# Tease Tomorrow's Issue the Day Before

## The Story

One of Chenell Basilio's most reliable subscriber engines is a single recurring post. The day before each *Growth in Reverse* issue goes out, she writes a teaser: "teasing the newsletter beforehand the day before I will write a little post." She is open that she didn't invent the format: "I did steal this format from Justin Walsh because he's like the master at teasing his newsletter." (Justin Welsh built the habit; Chenell copied it wholesale.)

The mechanics are simple. She pulls the single most interesting, most valuable beat from tomorrow's story and writes it as a strong hook, then follows with a line inviting people to join however many readers and not miss the issue tomorrow. The curiosity gap does the work: the teaser gives the payoff a name but withholds the story, and the only way to get it is to be subscribed when it lands. By her account these posts "usually get like 150 to 200 subscribers a week," a meaningful weekly lift for a newsletter her size.

The most important part is how she measures it. The teasers often don't rack up likes, and she has learned to ignore that entirely: "the pointless numbers are the likes but on the back end like the really impactful numbers are the subscribers." A post can look like a dud on vanity metrics and still be one of her best acquisition channels, in part because people bookmark it as a reminder to open the email, which she believes also helps its reach. She runs a companion play too, a recap thread of the previous deep dive ending in "go subscribe and I'll send you the issue now," but the pre-issue teaser is the dependable weekly driver.

## Lesson for Creators

Consistency plus a curiosity gap beats cleverness here. If you publish on a schedule, you have a free weekly promotion slot the day before: lead with the one best line from what's coming, name the payoff, withhold the rest, and point people to subscribe so they don't miss it. Then judge the post by subscribers gained, not likes, because the two often diverge and only one pays the rent. A teaser that flops on engagement can still be your highest-converting post of the week.
