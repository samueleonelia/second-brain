# The Cradle Video and the Accidental Dog Influencer Business

## The Story

Spotted By Humphrey wasn't planned. "In 2017 my wife and I brought home a French bulldog and we didn't plan on doing anything. We just basically created an Instagram account and we just started posting videos and photos. Obviously, our dog was really cute and we're a little biased, but he's a really good-looking French bulldog, right? So yeah, we just started doing that" (Source 1).

One specific video tipped it. "His videos started taking off, like one of the videos, it's like me cradling him like a baby and like you can see my torso like in that video. And that's been viewed over a million times. So my torso has gone viral along with Humphrey" (Source 1).

The pivot from petfluencer to e-commerce was demand-driven, not strategic. "And then this is like 2017. The space on Instagram wasn't as crowded as it is now. And his videos started taking off... after a certain point we're like, okay, because people were asking like, hey, like, where did you get that leash? Or where did you get that harness? And so instead of just telling them, hey, go here and buy it, we just thought, oh, we can do the same model that we did with Urban EDC. But cater it towards dog enthusiasts. And so that's what we did" (Source 1).

The Streamlined Solopreneur version of the same story: "in 2018, brought home a French bulldog named Humphrey. This is right around the time when Instagram was promoting videos. A few of his videos... Actually, what's funny is the videos where I'm holding him and cradling him like a baby, those really took off, like into the million views" (Source 2). "He gained a following really fast. It surprised me. We weren't planning on that, but it just happened. So my wife and I were like, 'All right, people are asking us like, Hey, what's that leash? What's the harness you have? So we decided, Hey, we have an eCommerce brand already with Urban EDC, my first company, why don't we just do the same thing but for French Bulldog owners?'" (Source 2).

Sandy, Yong-Soo's wife, operates Spotted By Humphrey. The combined Humphrey audience: "Humphrey and Pota, who have over 150,000 followers across all social channels under the name Spotted Humphrey" (Source 2). The business is also a brand-partnership engine: "I have one side, which is like, you know, I love this gear stuff. And then French bulldogs, I love spending time with my dogs. And so they're all like, part of this business now... we can also travel and like, we get a lot of sponsorship deals from five star hotels and they'll pay us to come up there. We stay there for free, you know, amazing food and all that stuff" (Source 3).

The business operates with separation by design. "So my wife is the one that runs the main petfluencer account. And so obviously, we talk about things and you know, whenever we have brand collaborations, I help out in the background, but like she's the voice of Humphrey and all that. And so she's technically the operator behind Spotted by Humphrey, which is the ecommerce store for French bulldogs and all that" (Source 3).

The professional-grade outcome — "Sandy and Humphrey were even invited down to Los Angeles for a segment on Shopify's YouTube channel. This was a professional studio set with camera crew, backstage make-up artists, and real actors and actresses on the set" (Source 2) — happened because a single Instagram video about cradling a dog crossed a million views.

## Lesson for Creators

The interesting part isn't that a viral video led to a business. It's that the Chungs were ready to convert because they had a model they could clone. They already ran Urban EDC. They knew how to source, list, fulfill, and run welcome flows for an e-commerce brand. When their audience started asking "where did you get the leash?", the time from question to functioning shop was short because the playbook was already on the shelf. For most creators the constraint isn't an audience or a viral moment — it's that when the moment comes, they don't yet have the operational scaffolding to convert it. Build the second business with the first business's machinery and the audience just decides which inventory you stock.
