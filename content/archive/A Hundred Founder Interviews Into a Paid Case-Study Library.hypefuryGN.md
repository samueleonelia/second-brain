# A Hundred Founder Interviews Into a Paid Case-Study Library

## The Story

Most founders never share the inside story of how they actually raised money. Houck's membership is built on closing that gap. He has interviewed more than 100 founders who collectively raised over $500 million from investors like Y Combinator, a16z, and Sequoia, and rather than letting each interview live and die as a podcast episode, he "turned those conversations into case studies," with new ones added every week.

That reframing is the mechanism. The same recording that feeds his free YouTube and podcast also becomes a written, searchable case study behind the paywall. The interview is the raw material; the case study is the asset. One conversation produces both reach (the public episode) and recurring value (the library entry a paying member can study later).

The library does not sit alone. Membership bundles it with weekly deep dives, his takeaways from each episode, a private founder community of 300-plus members, and "My database of over 1,800+ pitch decks." Each interview compounds the value of the whole bundle: the more founders he talks to, the more reasons there are to pay, and the more the back catalogue justifies the subscription on its own.

Because the catalogue grows every week, the membership gets more valuable over time without a new product launch. The work of recording interviews he was already doing for the audience quietly doubles as inventory for the paid tier.

## Lesson for Creators

An interview is not a disposable episode; it is raw material for an asset that can be sold again and again. Record the conversation once, then turn it into a written case study that lives in a library members pay to access. A back catalogue that grows every week becomes its own reason to subscribe, so the content you already make for reach does double duty as the product.
