# The Therapist's Reframe Hook

## The Story

A recurring structure in Abby Rawlinson's best-performing captions is a two-move reframe: state the problem the reader thinks they have, then reveal it as something else entirely, seen through her lens as a therapist. A June 2026 post opens: "Lots of people think they have a strategy problem." It then lists what they believe they need (a better plan, more confidence, more discipline, more consistency) before pivoting on her authority: "But as a therapist, I've noticed something fascinating. Many of the things people describe as business problems are actually nervous system patterns."

The move works because it does two things a plain tip cannot. First, it validates the reader's stated frustration before overturning it, so the correction lands as insight rather than contradiction. Second, the reframe can only be delivered by *her*: a marketer could give strategy tips, but relabeling business friction as a nervous-system pattern is a claim only a therapist is licensed to make. The hook and the differentiation are the same sentence.

She closes the loop by collapsing the reframed problem to a single human truth: "Because underneath every business is a human being." The post has taken a crowded topic (business strategy) and routed it back to her niche (the nervous system), so the content is simultaneously broad enough to travel and specific enough to be unmistakably hers.

This is why the format compounds reach rather than just earning a like. Each post teaches the audience the same underlying frame (whatever you think your problem is, look at the nervous system underneath it), which trains a distinct point of view across hundreds of posts. The reframe is the brand, repeated.

## Lesson for Creators

Don't open with the answer; open with the wrong assumption your reader already holds, then reframe it through the one lens only you can offer. The pattern is *you think it's X; from where I sit, it's actually Y.* It validates before it corrects, and it ties every post back to the single idea you want to own, so reach and positioning reinforce each other instead of competing.
