# Write Short-Form Videos as Poems

## The Story

Kyla Scanlon built an audience of over 650,000 followers in three years by explaining the Federal Reserve, inflation, and the housing market in sixty-second videos. The obvious explanation is topic selection. The real explanation is hidden in how the scripts are built.

On the Pathless Path podcast, Paul Millerd asked her how she thinks about the cadence and pace of her videos, whether it was trial and error or an actual philosophy. Her answer: _"I write them as poems"_. The scripts are structured as poems before they become videos, with deliberate beats, line lengths, and what she called poetic word timbre. Millerd's reaction on air was that he would never be able to unsee her TikToks as poems again, and that nobody knew this about her work.

She had the training for it. She grew up reading around ten books a week for over a decade and reads poetry and philosophy as a deliberate input alongside economic data. The poem format is the transfer of that reading habit into a production technique: a poem is the oldest technology for making dense information memorable and rhythmic, and a sixty-second explainer about monetary policy has exactly the same constraint.

The result reads as effortless speed. Viewers describe her videos as having the best information-per-second ratio on the internet, but the compression is engineered line by line at the script stage, not improvised at the camera.

## Lesson for Creators

The unit of craft in short-form video is the script, not the edit. Structuring a script as a poem forces three decisions that retention depends on: where the beats land, how long each line runs, and which word carries the stress. A creator in any dense niche can copy this mechanically by drafting the script in verse form first and only then recording it.
