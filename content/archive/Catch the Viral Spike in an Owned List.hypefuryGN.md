# Catch the Viral Spike in an Owned List

## The Story

The COVID essay hit hard. On Infinite Loops, Pueyo counts _"65 million reads and most of them within a week"_. A spike that size usually disappears: readers arrive, share, and leave, and the writer keeps nothing. Pueyo instead ran a capture mechanism inside the viral artifact. Per Growth In Reverse, he embedded a Google Form at the bottom of his Medium articles to collect emails while attention was live, picking the fastest tool over a polished one, then moved the list to Mailchimp and later to Substack in May 2021. That captured list is the owned asset; it became the Uncharted Territories newsletter, around 81,000 subscribers today.

The distinction matters. The same spike also took him from roughly 450 to over 35,000 Twitter *followers* in a month (per Growth In Reverse), but that is rented platform reach, not an asset he controls. The durable thing the spike produced was the email list the embedded form captured, the one channel he can reach again on his own terms.

## Lesson for Creators

Virality is a flood, not a faucet. Put an email capture inside the viral piece itself so a one-time spike becomes a list you can reach again. Attention you do not convert to an owned channel is attention you rent and lose.
