# One Deep Dive Many Spokes Best Saved for Paid

## The Story

Aakash Gupta organizes his whole week around producing one excellent paid newsletter deep dive, usually around 5,000 words making eleven or twelve distinct points. That single artifact is the hub. From it he spins spokes: because a social post only needs three or four of those points, one deep dive yields three or four LinkedIn or Twitter posts almost for free. The expensive research is done once; distribution is a matter of slicing it for each platform.

What makes the model hold together is the gating, which he sums up as _best most first_. The paid subscribers get the strongest, most complete material before anyone else. When he decides what to release for free or on social, he withholds enough that the paid tier still has a reason to exist. If a deep dive contains five strong infographics, maybe two go to free subscribers and onto social, and three stay behind the paywall. Give everything away and there is no reason to pay; give nothing away and there is no top of funnel. He runs the spokes as the free funnel and keeps the densest value as the paid core.

The mechanism is one act of creation feeding many acts of distribution, with a deliberate value gradient from free to paid. The repurposing buys reach; the gating protects revenue.

## Lesson for Creators

Make one deep thing, then cut it into many platform-native pieces instead of creating separately for each channel. The copyable rule is a value gradient: release enough of the work for free to pull people in, and withhold enough of the best so paying is still worth it. Repurposing without gating gives away the business; gating without repurposing starves the funnel.
