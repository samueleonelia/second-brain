# Spend on Retention Because Growth Is the Easy Part

## The Story

Most newsletter advice online is about the climb from zero to ten thousand subscribers. Geoff Sharpe thinks that is the wrong obsession. On the SparkLoop podcast (2024) he laid out a thesis that inverts the usual priority: _"it's actually never been easier to grow but it's never been harder to retain those people that you grow."_ Cheap paid ads and referral tools have commoditized acquisition; keeping people is where the real difficulty, and the real leverage, now sits.

The economics are what make it more than a slogan. If he can cut a subscriber's churn rate by 30 to 40%, that is revenue he gets to redeploy — every retained reader is one he doesn't have to re-acquire. So his instruction to operators fixated on list size is to flip it: stop _"worrying about growing your list worrying about retention"_ instead. Plow money into best-targeted Facebook ads to handle the growth mechanically, and spend the scarce human attention on the parts that keep people.

Concretely, retention for Lookout means a strong onboarding series, audience segmentation, ongoing surveys, and above all editorial good enough that readers don't leave. His summary of the tradeoff is an efficiency argument: focus less on the growth grind and more on retention, and _"your ad spend and your resources will go a lot farther."_ The same acquisition dollar simply buys more when fewer of the subscribers it bought are leaking out the bottom.

## Lesson for Creators

If acquisition is cheap and mechanical, it is no longer where your effort earns the most. Automate growth — buy it with well-targeted ads — and pour your genuinely scarce resource, attention, into retention: onboarding, segmentation, surveys, and editorial quality. A 30-40% cut in churn is money you get to spend again on growth, which means retention work quietly funds acquisition. Treat keeping subscribers, not getting them, as the hard and high-leverage problem.
