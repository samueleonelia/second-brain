# The Content Interview Beats Ghostwriting

## The Story

Most founders who hire out their LinkedIn presence want to disappear from the process entirely: hand it to an agency, a ghostwriter, or a Claude prompt, and never think about it again. Tommy Clark, founder of Compound Content Studio and writer of the Social Files newsletter, argues that this is exactly why so much founder content dies flat. His rule is "delegate but not abdicate." As he puts it, "you can build systems around content creation, but the thing is if you're completely removing yourself, you're going to likely remove the aspects of that content that make it unique and engaging."

The system he uses to keep the founder in without eating their week is the Content Interview. Rather than asking the founder to write, Compound records a 30-60 minute conversation with them roughly every two weeks. Beforehand the team preps a list of 7-10 prompts, and each prompt is engineered to translate into a single post, which keeps the founder from rambling and gives the interviewer clear targets to press on. The founder just talks; the transcript becomes the raw material for the next one to two weeks of posts. From one interview the team produces roughly 5-10 posts, adding the hooks, structure, and formatting.

Tommy's reasoning is that the timeline is now flooded with generic, AI-assembled posts, so the only durable way to stand out is founder-specific: "the main way you stand out on the timeline is through your own personal stories and your own real opinions." A ghostwriter starting from a blank page cannot manufacture those; an interview mines them directly. The founder's total time cost lands around two to three hours a week even when working with the agency, versus the five-to-fifteen hours writing solo would take.

The non-negotiable is that the ideas and stories originate from the founder. The team acts as sculptor working a marble block that the founder supplied, not as an author inventing a person. "you need to remain involved enough that your actual thoughts and opinions and stories are the ones making into the content," Tommy says, which is the line between delegating production and abdicating identity.

## Lesson for Creators

If you are too busy to write but your personal brand is the growth channel, do not hire someone to imagine your voice. Hire someone to extract it. Sit for a recorded interview on a fixed cadence, come with 7-10 questions each mapped to one post, and let an editor turn the transcript into finished pieces. You keep the stories and opinions that make the content un-copyable; you outsource only the formatting. Time in: a couple hours every two weeks. Output: one to two weeks of posts that still sound like you.
