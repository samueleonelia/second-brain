# Befriend Five Creators to Clear the Initial Barrier

## The Story

In his June 2022 essay *Cheat, Pray, Work* Jakob Greenfeld starts from an observation anyone can replicate: successful Twitter accounts all seem to have 5-10 accounts that immediately engage with whatever they post. Most people who notice this reach the wrong conclusion and try to assemble an engagement pod. Jakob's diagnosis is that those first responders are real friends, and the pod people are copying the symptom instead of the cause.

The mechanical layer underneath: platforms like Twitter and TikTok initially show a post to a tiny percentage of followers, and only promising engagement in that test window earns wider reach. Reddit, YouTube and Product Hunt work similarly. Jakob quantifies the threshold: "it usually only takes around 5-20 upvotes to get over the initial barrier", and on Product Hunt or Hacker News "10 upvotes within the first hour after posting is usually enough to make it to the front page". The barrier exists to filter spam, which means clearing it is a precondition for any content strategy, not an optimization.

He names three ways to get those first upvotes: Cheat (engagement groups, which platforms eventually punish and which strip out real market feedback because members upvote anything), Pray (make great content and hope), and Work, the one he endorses. The Work playbook is specific: identify a handful of creators whose content you genuinely like, then "help them wherever you can without expecting anything in return". That means giving feedback, buying their products, leaving reviews, inviting them to events, sharing their work, and setting up notifications or a dedicated list so you actually see their posts (by default the algorithm hides most of them from you too).

Over time the favor returns without being asked for: when you launch or post something big, your creator friends are the first to comment, buy and upvote, which is exactly the early signal the algorithm wants. Unlike a pod, friends still refuse to share weak work, so you keep honest feedback, and because nothing is coordinated the platforms have nothing to punish. Jakob closes the essay with the principle: "Stop thinking about impersonal seas of eyeballs or ways to trick the algorithm. Start building genuine relationships instead."

## Lesson for Creators

The distribution game on every user-generated-content platform is decided in the first hour by a countable number of interactions, usually under twenty. You can rent that early engagement (pods, spam DMs) and pay for it in penalties and self-deception, or you can pre-earn it by spending months being materially useful to a small set of peers. The second route compounds: the same relationships that clear the algorithm's barrier also bring ideas, encouragement and collaborations.
