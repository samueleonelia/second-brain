# A Made-By Badge That Landed a Podcast Shoutout

## The Story

In November 2020 Jakob Greenfeld was four weeks into selling his first paid product, a Gumroad analytics database called Gum Spy, and had roughly 600 Twitter followers. He had hesitated over one tiny design decision: a small badge in the lower-right corner of the page saying the project was made by him. The argument against it was that the badge instantly marks a project as an indie effort, which can read as less professional. He shipped it anyway, on all of his pages, because he liked seeing such badges on other makers' sites. He thought of it as a "membership card for the indie maker club".

Then one day he started getting waves of new Twitter followers and Gumroad subscription notifications without having tweeted anything. A DM from Trevor McKendrick explained it: Sam Parr and Shaan Puri had discussed him on their podcast (My First Million, an entrepreneurship show with hundreds of thousands of listeners). In the episode, Shaan said he discovered Jakob's work because of the tiny badge on the Gum Spy landing page. Without it the site would have been an anonymous project. With it, Shaan could trace the page to a "build in public type of guy" running a public learning experiment with several similar launches, and decided he was worth a shoutout.

Jakob's own accounting of the outcome, in his essay about the episode: "I really made dozens of sales (still counting) and got hundreds of new Twitter followers and newsletter subscribers only thanks to the tiny badge." He also stresses the starting position that makes the number meaningful: "I’m just a guy with 600 Twitter followers who launched his first paid product 4 weeks ago." In a later interview on the Louis and Kyle Show he pointed to the same episode as the payoff of keeping his products visibly attached to his name, even when an individual product does not earn much by itself.

The acquisition step costs nothing. The badge code Jakob used is freely available (he credits Pieter Levels) and takes minutes to add. What it buys is that every product page, however small, becomes an entry point into the creator's whole ecosystem rather than a dead end.

## Lesson for Creators

If you run multiple small projects, the projects themselves are a distribution network for you as a person, but only if each one is signed. A visible made-by badge converts anonymous product traffic into followers and subscribers, and it makes you legible to exactly the high-leverage people (podcasters, newsletter writers, curators) whose job is finding interesting makers to talk about. The mechanic is asymmetric: near-zero cost, small steady trickle in the median case, and occasionally a single discovery event that outperforms months of posting.
