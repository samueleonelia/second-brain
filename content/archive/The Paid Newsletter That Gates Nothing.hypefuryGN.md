# The Paid Newsletter That Gates Nothing

## The Story

Kyla Scanlon's Substack has paid tiers, $10 a month, $110 a year, or pay what you want. What it does not have is a paywall. Every post is public. On her About page she states it plainly: the newsletter is where the thinking for all of her work happens, and _"It will always be free"_.

The pitch to paying readers is not access but independence. In her words, paid support means _"I don't have to optimize for clicks or take every brand deal that comes my way"_. The reader is buying her freedom to say no, and the absence of a paywall is what makes the offer credible: a creator who gates content has already started optimizing for conversion.

Growth In Reverse noted how rare this is in the creator space and how it shapes everything downstream. With no premium tier to feed, every essay can circulate at full reach, get screenshotted, shared on LinkedIn, and quoted by Bloomberg, which is exactly the distribution a finance commentator needs. The book deal, the speaking fees, and the single-sponsor arrangement with Public monetize the reputation the free essays build.

## Lesson for Creators

A paywall monetizes your best readers and silences your best marketing at the same time, because the gated essays are the ones that would have traveled. The patronage structure flips it: content stays at maximum reach, and the paid offer sells something a paywall cannot, the writer's independence. It works when the surrounding business (books, speaking, sponsorship) is what converts the reach into income.
