# Growth Playbooks Decay With Every Copying Cohort

## The Story

Asked why aspiring indie hackers cannot simply retrace Pieter Levels's steps, Jakob Greenfeld gave a structural answer in the Indie MBA conversation: if you try to do what Peter did at the time, "it simply won't work anymore" because it has been overdone. Each cohort that copies a tactic gets weaker returns than the one before; in his words, "you will only get like a fraction of the results a previous generation saw".

He itemizes how the classic indie playbook decayed. The free-traffic hack of relaunching on Product Hunt every month with each new feature (Levels relaunched Nomad List with new filters as version 7.0) died when Product Hunt imposed roughly six-month gaps between launches of the same product. Build-in-public revenue-sharing stopped being catchy once everyone did it, because "the same amount of attention gets split between a lot of people". And the PR channel that made first movers famous (magazines covering the novelty, feeding SEO backlinks) only ever existed for the first movers; by definition it does not survive replication.

The flip side is that new waves keep forming, and riding one early is the same mechanic in reverse. Jakob describes friends who "had like 2 000 followers and now they have a hundred thousand followers" on Twitter simply because they jumped hard onto the NFT hype train and built from there. He is candid that he personally cannot do this (he is too skeptical by nature to board hype trains with genuine enthusiasm), and he saw the same dynamic in academia, where the career rewards went to whoever loudly took each statistical fluke seriously before it evaporated. The people who win the wave are the ones who can commit to it without irony while it lasts.

## Lesson for Creators

Every documented growth tactic carries an invisible timestamp. Before copying one, ask what cohort you would be joining: first movers got PR, backlinks and novelty attention that fourth-cohort imitators structurally cannot get, and platforms patch the loopholes the first cohort exploited. The practical options are to hunt for tactics that have not yet been written up, or to enter a rising wave early and accept its crash risk. Copying last generation's playbook at full price is the one option the math rules out.
