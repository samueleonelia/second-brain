# Build the Free List Before Switching On Paid

## The Story

When Aakash Gupta lays out how to build a paid newsletter, the order matters more than any single tactic. He did not start charging early. He grew distribution on one social channel first, then built a free email list of roughly 60,000 subscribers, and only after that did he turn on paid. The free phase was not a holding pattern. It was where he learned the craft that paid subscribers later pay for: how to write a subject line people open, how to write the first three paragraphs so readers continue, and how to pace sends so unsubscribes do not outrun growth.

The sequencing protects against the most common failure he sees, which is monetizing before there is anything to monetize. A creator who flips on payments at 2,000 subscribers is asking a tiny, untested audience to fund a craft the creator has not yet learned. By waiting until 60,000 free readers had taught him what worked, Aakash entered the paid phase already knowing which formats and topics earned attention, so the only new variable was willingness to pay.

He frames the paid step as a distinct decision that needs its own differentiated offer, not a switch you throw the moment a list exists. The free list is the proving ground; paid is the layer you add once the ground is solid.

## Lesson for Creators

Grow and learn on a free list before you charge, and treat the size of that list as evidence you have learned the craft, not just as a vanity number. The copyable rule is a strict order of operations: one distribution channel, then a free list large enough to teach you what converts, then paid. Monetizing a thin, untested audience is how creators burn the goodwill they will need later.
