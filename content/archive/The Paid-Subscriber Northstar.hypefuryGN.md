# The Paid-Subscriber Northstar

## The Story

Aakash Gupta runs Product Growth like a product, and a product has exactly one north-star metric. His is paid subscribers. Not the 166,000 free subscribers a host once recited back to him, and not the 250,000 LinkedIn followers. The number he optimizes is net new paid subscribers, which he reads as paid signups minus paid cancels on every single send. He is blunt that some emails come back negative, where more people cancel their paid subscription than join, and he treats each of those as a forensic case: was it the hook, the subject line, the depth of the deep dive, the infographics, the amount of research.

Because that metric is fixed, he can look back at every post he has ever shipped and ask what actually drove it, sorting by topic, timeliness, and angle rather than by what felt good to write. He calls his content calendar his primary product. It is a spreadsheet of upcoming titles and publish dates that he watches closely, steering future pieces toward whatever has most recently moved paid conversions. The free list and the follower count are inputs he is happy to grow, but they never get a vote on what to make next.

The discipline is in refusing the vanity metrics that are easier to grow. Free subscribers and followers climb with reach; paid subscribers climb only when the work is worth money. By judging himself against the harder number, he keeps the whole operation pointed at the thing that pays.

## Lesson for Creators

Pick the one metric closest to revenue and let it, not applause, decide what you publish next. The copyable move is to log every send against net paid conversions and to run the losers as autopsies rather than forgetting them. Vanity counts feel like progress, but a creator who optimizes the number that pays builds a business instead of an audience.
