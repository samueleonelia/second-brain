# Sub-2-Dollar Subscribers Once the Content Proved Its Legs

## The Story

When Houck's newsletter ramped from near-zero to about $50,000 a month in a few months, the interviewer asked how he seeded that growth. Houck started with what he had: a modest X and LinkedIn audience of about 20,000 each at the start, plus collaborations and cross-promos with peers, a lot of which happened quietly in DMs and group chats.

Then he named the part most creators hide. The dirty secret of big newsletters, he said, is that "most scale with paid ads" on Facebook, Instagram, and now X, sometimes TikTok. With good creative and targeting you can acquire a subscriber for under $2. Against a lifetime value of roughly $12 a year, that is a 6:1 return, an engine that needs no venture money to run.

The sequencing is the lesson, not the ads. Houck did not open with a paid budget. He "invested modestly after proving legs with content" and cross-promos first. Paid acquisition only amplifies a funnel that already converts organically; switch it on before the content and onboarding work and you are paying to pour subscribers into a leaky bucket.

Because the unit economics held, he had no interest in raising for the business. A 6:1 ratio that you can fund from cash flow is a reason to keep full ownership, not to take outside capital.

## Lesson for Creators

Paid ads are how most large newsletters actually scale, and a sub-$2 subscriber against a $12 value is a profitable, repeatable machine. But prove the organic funnel first. Earn some subscribers with content and cross-promos, confirm they stay and convert, and only then spend to amplify what is already working.
