# Generate LinkedIn Posts From a Growing Reference Folder

## The Story

Duncan Rogoff's third skill writes the actual LinkedIn posts, and two things keep its output from reading like generic AI. The first is the tone-of-voice file it pulls from, so every draft sounds like him. The second is a reference folder of posts he admires, which the skill reads as examples before it writes.

The reference folder is the part most people miss. Whenever he sees a post he likes, he tells the system to save it: add this to the list. Over time the examples accumulate, and the quality compounds. _"the skill is going to get better over time because it has stronger references."_ The skill isn't static; it's a library he keeps feeding.

He's deliberate about what he asks it to write. Giveaways grow a list, but they don't build trust on their own. _"You don't just want to be hammering people with giveaways, you actually want to establish yourself as an authority."_ So a chunk of his output is authority posts built on personal stories, the kind of thing only he can write. One he drafted this way opened: _"Your biggest asset on LinkedIn is the story you're too scared to tell."_ It plugs straight into daily life: a thought or a good conversation becomes a post in minutes.

## Lesson for Creators

An AI writing skill is only as good as what it reads. Feed it your voice and a growing library of posts you genuinely admire, and its drafts improve every week instead of plateauing. Then point it at authority and story, not just giveaways, because the posts only you could write are the ones that build trust.
