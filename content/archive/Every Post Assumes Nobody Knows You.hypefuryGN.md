# Every Post Assumes Nobody Knows You

## The Story

Adam Robinson treats the hook as the single most important part of a LinkedIn post, and he enforces it operationally: working with his consultant Alec, they will not even begin writing a post until the hook is nailed, because if the hook fails, no one reads the rest and the effort is wasted. He reaches for a print-media analogy, that a journalist historically spent half their time on the headline and half on the article, and applies the same split to social.

The reason the hook has to carry so much weight is a piece of data about his own audience. A follower named Peter Confori once analyzed Adam's whole audience and found the overlap between the readers of one post and the next was small, in his words _"the crossover of audience from post to post is like 15%"_ (Ahrefs Podcast). Combined with the fact that LinkedIn is non-sequential and can serve someone a six-month-old post first, this produces his core rule: _"you have to treat each post as though no one has ever heard of you before"_ (Ahrefs Podcast). Every post must re-establish credibility from zero.

He gives concrete hook patterns. The first is quantification: _"use as many numbers as humanly possible"_ (Ahrefs Podcast), because LinkedIn rewards them, especially numbers tied to money, revenue, and profit. The second is the credibility statement, an opener like having bootstrapped $3M ARR three times, each time twice as fast with half the resources, which most readers have not done and which stacks authority. The third, for people without those war stories, is what he calls brokering information, an Oprah-style move. His own example opens with _"I spoke to 10 early stage founders over the last four weeks about cold outbound"_ (Ahrefs Podcast) and then lays out the four things they all do that fails and the one that works. You borrow authority by aggregating other people's experience.

## Lesson for Creators

Assume every reader is a stranger, every time, and design the first line to prove you are worth reading before they know anything about you. Lead with a number, a credibility marker, or brokered insight. And impose the discipline Adam does: if you cannot write a compelling hook, do not write the post at all. The hook is not decoration on the idea; it is the gate the idea has to pass through.
