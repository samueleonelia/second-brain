# Win by Assembling Experts, Not Being One

## The Story

When COVID broke in March 2020, Tomas Pueyo was not an epidemiologist. His move was to read everything scattered across papers, dashboards, and threads and consolidate it into one roughly 39-page explainer on why the world had to act now. He told Rebel Wisdom that people should not listen to him because of who he is, since he is not a biologist. What he had done, he said, was to "gather all the research from all these different places and put it in one place so that people can understand it," and readers should judge it on the data and sources, not on him.

He framed his own role precisely. He was not the person making the arguments but the one "making the Assembly of the arguments." The piece reached tens of millions of readers and, per Growth In Reverse, seeded the audience that became the Uncharted Territories newsletter. The authority came from synthesis, not credentials.

## Lesson for Creators

You do not need to be the world expert to own a topic. Be the person who reads everything, strips the jargon, and assembles it into one place a normal reader can follow. The synthesizer who makes a confusing subject legible often out-distributes the experts who produced the raw material.
