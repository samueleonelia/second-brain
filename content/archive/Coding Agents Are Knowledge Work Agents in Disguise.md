# Coding Agents Are Knowledge Work Agents in Disguise

Codex looks like an AI coding tool, and if you are not an engineer the natural conclusion is that it is not for you. Katie Parrott opens her Every guide by saying that reading is exactly what makes most knowledge workers miss what Codex actually is: a tool-using agentic workspace that happens to be very good at code, but can read your files, drive your apps and browser, run multi-step tasks, check its own work, and keep a persistent goal across a session.

Her opening vignette makes the reframe concrete. A request for a launch plan arrives on Monday morning. The agent runs in the cloud or on a Mac Mini you keep awake at home. While you commute, it reads the relevant Slack threads, pulls customer notes out of Google Drive, checks last quarter's numbers in PostHog, and starts drafting a go-to-market plan in Notion. Nothing in that workflow is code. The agent's "coding" surface area, files plus shell plus a browser plus connected apps, is just the general-purpose substrate of knowledge work.

The practical implication for non-developers is to stop filtering out coding-agent tooling. Skills, slash commands, goals, subagents, CLAUDE.md, plan mode, verification loops, all of these are operating-system primitives for working with an agent. The fact that they were originally built for engineers does not make them engineering features; it makes them battle-tested.
