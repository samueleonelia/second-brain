# Experiment on Top of a Steady Foundation

## The Story

The habit that carried Olivia Wickstrom from zero to 21,000 subscribers is not a single tactic, it is a posture. Once her foundation was steady, consistent posting, clear pillars, a real point of view, she kept layering new experiments on top of it, even ones that might not work. Notes, Pinterest, live recordings: each was a bet placed on top of the base, and a few reshaped the whole publication.

She runs it with rules learned by doing it wrong first. Each experiment ties to a pillar or a goal, she decides how she will measure success before starting, and she refuses to call it too early: _"Give it at least three to six months before you decide if it's working"_. The writers who plateau, she argues, are the ones who stop adding experiments once the foundation is set.

## Lesson for Creators

Consistency gets you to a plateau; experiments get you past it. Keep a stable base, then layer one new, measured bet on top at a regular cadence and give it real time before you judge it.
