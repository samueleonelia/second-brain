# The Voice Note to Published Post Engine

## The Story

The reason most people stop posting is friction: sitting down to write is a task you can always skip. Duncan Rogoff removed the task. He built an automation where the only human action is leaving a voice memo or a text: _"no matter where you are, you can leave a voice message, you can send a text, and it will go ahead and perform additional research on whatever it is that you want to post about that day."_ From there the system transcribes the note, researches the topic, writes the post through his viral-post framework, and publishes it to LinkedIn. End to end, _"And that took what 30 seconds or something like that."_

A deliberate design choice is that the system researches before it writes, because a raw thought is too thin to post well: _"we actually need to do additional research. And this is cool because it's actually going to make your post better, especially if you start bringing in statistics and things like that."_ The finished posts pull in fresh figures the author never personally looked up, which is what separates them from a generic hot take.

The real payoff is that publishing stops being an event and becomes ambient: _"anywhere on the go, if you have an idea for a topic, if you're talking to a friend, if you hear some crazy news, you can always have access to publish a viral LinkedIn post."_

## Lesson for Creators

Consistency is an engineering problem, not a willpower problem. When the distance between "I had a thought" and "it's published" collapses to a 30-second voice note, you post far more often. And bolting a research step in front of the writer means each automated post arrives sharper and more cited than an off-the-cuff one would have.
