# The 8K Subscriber Sponsorship Threshold

## The Story

Yong-Soo waited until 8,000 newsletter subscribers before accepting his first sponsor. "Started selling sponsors for my newsletter, around 8k subs. We just started getting more inbound requests for sponsorships!" (Source 1).

The framing of why he waited longer than most: "I wanted to be very picky about sponsors. I don't want to bring on any sponsors that pay me. It's all a deliberate practice of partnering up with brands that I personally enjoy and use" (Source 1). "Those brands take time to build relationships, etc., so that's why it's taken a bit of time!" (Source 1).

His own approach to outreach is patient and indirect: "Most of them came to me! As I said, I'm pretty picky about who I want to work with, so it's been fun seeing some brands that I personally use come to me. Also, I'm not afraid of asking brands I like whether or not they're open to sponsorship. The worst they can say is 'no.'" (Source 1).

The longer game uses the podcast as the relationship machine in front of sponsor sales: "I would urge folks to build relationships with decision-makers at these companies. For example, I have a podcast and I can interview the founders – this is a great way to warm up the relationship for a few months before asking for a sponsorship" (Source 1).

The SparkLoop podcast episode where Yong-Soo discusses sponsorship and the future of newsletters as a business covers the same architecture under "Growth Levers for Newsletters" — the show frames Yong-Soo's approach as part of his Five Growth Levers thesis (Source 2).

## Lesson for Creators

The default newsletter advice is to start monetizing early — and for ad-driven businesses with high CPMs it's correct. Yong-Soo's choice is the opposite: delay monetization until the audience is large enough that the founder can be picky about sponsors. The math is different when sponsor selection is signaling to the reader. A bad sponsor at 1K subscribers might net you $200 and a credibility hit you'll spend a year recovering from; at 8K subscribers with inbound demand, the founder can choose only brands they actually use, which compounds trust rather than spending it. The deeper lever is the podcast: interviewing a founder for an hour is a far better warm-up to a sponsorship conversation than a cold pitch, and most creators don't realize they're sitting on the highest-value relationship-building format ever invented. The 8K number isn't universal — it's the size at which his particular niche flipped from outbound to inbound. The principle is to find that flip in your own list before you start saying yes.
