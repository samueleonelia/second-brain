# Inbound-Led Outbound Through the Profile

## The Story

Adam Robinson's term for his whole model is "inbound-led outbound," and the point of building the audience is what it does to outbound response rates. His argument starts from a bleak premise: cold outbound response rates are trending toward zero because automation floods inboxes and filters block the flood. What survives that environment, he believes, is outreach from someone the recipient already feels they know. A salesy email from Adam gets answered because people feel a relationship with him; the same email from an unknown teammate does not.

The sharpest part of the mechanic is that the founder does not have to be on the call. When Adam messages a prospect from his profile saying a teammate will run the discovery, the result, in his words, is _"a 100% book rate and 100% show rate"_ (Maja Voje, GTM Strategist Podcast). The parasocial trust attaches to the introduction, not to who takes the meeting. That is what makes it a growth engine rather than a founder bottleneck: _"the profile can be scaled beyond the hours that I have in the day"_ (Maja Voje, GTM Strategist Podcast), and he says that observation, that the effect was so scalable, is a big reason he is so motivated to make the brand as large as possible.

The discipline around it is restraint. The RB2B tool resolves anonymous website visitors to a LinkedIn profile and email, but Adam is emphatic that you do not open with "I see you visited my website." The rule, his phrase, is that you never pitch-slap them; you reach out within a few hours of the visit and run the exact same respectful outbound playbook you would run for any warm lead.

## Lesson for Creators

An audience is not just reach, it is a permission structure for outbound. The people who consume your content will take a meeting from you that they would never take from a stranger, and crucially they will take it even when someone else runs the call. Build the parasocial trust first, then let it book meetings at rates cold outreach cannot touch, and scale it by decoupling the introduction from the person who actually shows up.
