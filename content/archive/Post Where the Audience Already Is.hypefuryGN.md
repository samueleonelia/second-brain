# Post Where the Audience Already Is

## The Story

In March 2021 Aakash Gupta spent a weekend researching and writing an argument that Roblox could become the Shopify of gaming, and published it on his own website. Nobody came. For the level of work it was a flat disappointment, because an owned site with no audience is a stage with no seats. So he took the same piece and posted it to LinkedIn, where he had never really posted before, and the reaction was immediate: old contacts surfaced, connection requests flooded in, and the response was enough to light the habit that became Product Growth. The content did not change. The venue did.

The lesson he drew was about cold-start mechanics, not writing quality. Distribution does not appear because the work is good; it appears because you publish where people already gather and the platform already pushes content to them. His website could not introduce him to anyone. LinkedIn could. He kept the deep research as the substance but moved the act of publishing to where reach was free, and only later funneled those readers back to an asset he owned.

The order is the whole point. Owned platforms are where you keep an audience; rented platforms with native distribution are where you find one. Trying to start on the owned platform is publishing into a void.

## Lesson for Creators

Do your best work, then release it where an algorithm will hand it to strangers, not where it sits waiting to be discovered. The copyable move is to treat your website or newsletter as the destination and a high-reach social platform as the on-ramp, especially at the start when you have no list to fall back on. Good work in an empty room is still an empty room.
