# Rent a Media Company's Growth Engine to Scale a Newsletter

## The Story

When Tommy Clark launched the Social Files newsletter in 2022 while working at Triple Whale, he had almost no distribution. In his words, "I started social files proper back in 2022," and at that point he "didn't really have an audience at the time." A newsletter with, per the Creator Spotlight profile of him, roughly 500 subscribers.

The unlock was not a growth hack he ran himself; it was a partnership. A friend, Daniel Murray of the Marketing Millennials newsletter, had been picked up by the media company Workweek, and Workweek happened to be looking for someone to write specifically about social media marketing. Daniel made the introduction, Tommy had a conversation with Workweek's CEO, and they signed him. Workweek's model, as the Creator Spotlight profile describes it, is to find talent producing content in a business niche and then grow that audience using a recommendation network and paid advertising, monetizing later through ad sales. Under that engine, Social Files added subscribers by the thousands, eventually passing 17,000 on the email list (per Creator Spotlight).

The trade was clean division of labor: Workweek supplied the distribution machine and ran monetization, and Tommy supplied the writing. "they did all the ad sales I just had to create content and do what I do best," he says. He ran with Workweek for about a year and a half before going independent again, taking the grown list with him. The lesson is not that partnerships are free (he gave up ad revenue and some control), but that a creator with a strong niche voice and no audience can borrow an established operator's acquisition system instead of grinding subscriber-by-subscriber alone.

## Lesson for Creators

If your writing is good but your distribution is zero, the fastest path may be to plug into someone who has already built the growth and monetization machine. Media networks, larger newsletters, and recommendation-driven publishers are actively hunting for niche talent to feed their engine. The price is a revenue share and some editorial independence; the return is thousands of subscribers you could not have reached solo, and often the right to keep the audience if you later leave. Warm introductions from peers already inside those networks are how these deals actually happen, so invest in relationships with creators one step ahead of you.
