# The Creator Funnel Built by Two

## The Story

Dave and his wife run their management-training business as what he calls a classic creator funnel. At the top is a social presence on LinkedIn and Twitter; that feeds an email newsletter, the MGMT Playbook (its homepage lists 50,000+ subscribers and 175+ issues, published weekly on Wednesdays); the newsletter feeds their cohort-based courses; and the courses lead to a roster of about eight CEOs he coaches one-to-one. He describes the sequence plainly: "Twitter and Linkedin lead to the newsletter which lead to the courses," with coaching at the deep end. Building the newsletter also reduced platform risk, since Twitter or LinkedIn "can turn me off at any moment in time."

The wedge underneath it is a pair of statistics they kept hitting: per Kline, about 60% of managers fail (new managers, middle managers, and senior executives alike, across industries and countries), yet only about 15% of managers get any training, roughly one in six people getting an hour of training. Their whole premise is filling that gap, drawing on careers at companies (Bridgewater and Moody's for him, Goldman Sachs and Google for her) that poured millions into developing leaders.

Their most counterintuitive move was positioning. They initially assumed running the business as a married couple would be a liability and tried to hide it: "the husband wife Duo thing would be a bug and then it turned out to be a feature." Two people who speak the same language but see the workplace differently, with his framework-and-writing role and her sales-and-human-connection role, plus the fact that they gently pick on each other, made the brand approachable and authentic. They now run it "like a dinner party." The division of labor is explicit: she owns business development and about a third of the teaching, he does the writing and about two-thirds of the teaching.

## Lesson for Creators

A durable creator business is a funnel, not a single channel: rented attention (social) feeds an owned list (newsletter) that feeds a real product (courses) that feeds your highest-touch offer (coaching). Anchor the whole thing to a sharp, quantified problem your audience already feels. And the "flaw" you're tempted to hide, like an unconventional two-person setup, is often the exact thing that makes you distinctive: lean into it instead of sanding it off.
