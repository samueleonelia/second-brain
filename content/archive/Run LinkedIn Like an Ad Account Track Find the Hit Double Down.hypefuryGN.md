# Run LinkedIn Like an Ad Account Track Find the Hit Double Down

## The Story

Andy Mewborn's mental model for content comes straight from paid media. He ran Facebook ads and noticed the operators who win aren't the ones with clever targeting — the platform automates that — they're the ones who test the most creative. "the people that experiment the most are going to win the most," he says on the *Bridge the Gap* podcast, calling constant experimentation one of Distribute's cultural checkboxes.

So he treats LinkedIn like an ad account. Every post is measured, not just published: "every time I post a LinkedIn post we have a spreadsheet of like okay what did this do what did this work." The spreadsheet turns posting from guesswork into a controlled test — you always know which format is your current champion and what you're testing against it. The same discipline runs his cold email, where he keeps one campaign he knows converts as a control and runs two more against it; if a challenger beats the six-month champion, the champion gets replaced.

The payoff is speed of exploitation. On the *Colin Cadmus* podcast he describes finding a winning format — video posts built around templates — and moving immediately: "we found a hit on Monday and that same day we just said all right we're doubling down on this and now we're doubling down on it until it doesn't work again." He doesn't reinvent each week; he recycles proven structures — "recycled of what we know already works" — the way a media buyer scales the ad that already converts. The result, in his telling, is that the winning template posts drove "a bunch of users" to Distribute.

## Lesson for Creators

Stop guessing which posts work. Keep a running spreadsheet of every post and its numbers so you always have a control and a challenger. When something outperforms, don't admire it — double down that same day, and keep milking the format until the data says it's dead. Most people build systems only for things that already work; the edge is having a system to keep experimenting, then ruthlessly recycling the winners instead of starting from a blank page each time.
