# Buy the First Year of Growth in Month Three

## The Story

Geoff Sharpe grew Lookout Media's local-news newsletters almost entirely with paid ads, and he is blunt that the fashionable advice to grind organic growth first is, for him, the wrong move. On the SparkLoop podcast (2024) he said the amount of organic growth Lookout focused on was absolutely minimal, while its open rates ran 58%-plus with a 12% clickthrough rate. His rule: _"if you have the money to be able to spend on paid ads that is what I would spend almost all"_ resources on. By his own account, over 90% of the audience came from paid growth.

The reasoning is a timeline argument, not a channel preference. He reframes growth as a time-frame problem: _"if your revenue is a function of the size of your list and the engagement of your list"_ then every month you delay paid acquisition is a month of compounding you never get back. The vivid version: _"if you can take your 20,000 list that you have at the end of the year and put that at month three"_ you buy yourself the extra revenue and runway that a bigger, earlier list produces. Spending a would-be growth-marketer's salary on Meta ads is, to him, just pulling that list-size curve forward in time.

There is a gate on the strategy. He only pours money in once the idea is validated — a hundred dollars of ads producing strong engagement is the green light. And the caveat he repeats is that editorial has to be genuinely good, because paid acquisition only pays off if the people you buy stick around. Local audiences, he found, are unusually engaged compared with other paid channels, which makes the math work.

## Lesson for Creators

If you have a validated offer and a way to monetize, audience size is partly a purchasing decision, not only a patience decision. Treat the first year of list growth as something you can compress by spending: a small paid test proves engagement, then you scale spend to move your end-of-year subscriber number to month three. The organic grind (Reddit posts, cold outreach, referral loops) is the fallback for when you lack the cash, not the default. The precondition is retention: buying subscribers only compounds if the editorial keeps them, so the engagement bar comes before the ad spend.
