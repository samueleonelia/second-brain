# Skip the Link Keep the Reach

## The Story

When Kyla Scanlon promotes a new essay in an Instagram Reel, the one thing missing from the post is the link. Growth In Reverse documented the pattern: she delivers the short-form version of the piece, mentions at the end that the full essay exists, and deliberately leaves the post link-free so the platform does not suppress its reach.

Instead of a link, she leaves a breadcrumb. She comments on her own post saying the piece can be found on Substack, knowing that a viewer who actually wants it can Google her name plus Substack and land there in seconds. In TikToks she does a variant of the same move, flashing a screenshot of the newsletter on screen while talking about the piece, again with no link attached.

The trade is explicit: she sacrifices the low-intent click for full algorithmic distribution. The viewers who search anyway are exactly the ones who convert into subscribers, while the post itself reaches the maximum number of new people who have never heard of her.

## Lesson for Creators

Platforms tax outbound links with reduced reach, and most creators pay that tax on every promotional post. The replicable move is to split the job in two: let the post carry the idea at full reach, and let a searchable breadcrumb (a self-comment, an on-screen screenshot, a memorable name) carry the high-intent traffic. Reach for the many, friction only for the motivated.
