# Stop Copying AI Workflows From People With Unlimited Tokens

## Idea
Workflow advice inherits the cost structure of the person giving it: agent loops are rational for insiders with no token budget and a slot machine for anyone on a $20 or $100 plan, so check whether you share the budget before copying the workflow.

- Attribution: Ras Mic on Greg Isenberg's Startup Ideas Podcast. The $1.3M figure refers to Peter Steinberger's tweet about one month of token burn (dollars' worth of tokens, as cited in the episode).
- Mic is explicit that Boris and Peter aren't malicious and their position makes sense: they need to experiment with self-healing agents and tokens cost them nothing. The problem is the advice cascading down to retail plans where one fire-and-forget loop eats the month's budget.
- Greg's image: loops without checkpoints are a slot machine. The legitimate exception: throwaway prototypes where any outcome is acceptable (Mic's Among Us simulator for benchmarking which models lie best, built in ~90 minutes, details wrong but irrelevant).
- Angle for the essay: this generalizes beyond loops. Every workflow you see on X embeds the author's prices, team size, and risk tolerance. The question is never "does it work?" but "does it work at my cost structure?" Same filter applies to SaaS stacks and ad strategies.
