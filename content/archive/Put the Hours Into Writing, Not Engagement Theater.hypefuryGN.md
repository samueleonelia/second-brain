# Put the Hours Into Writing, Not Engagement Theater

## The Story

A whole genre of LinkedIn advice sells intricate engagement routines: spreadsheets of accounts to comment on, hours of daily replying, an *exact engagement workflow to copy to get all these leads*. Tommy Clark runs almost the opposite, and says so explicitly: this "goes counter to a lot of the advice you'll hear." His allocation rule is that the content itself is the lever, so that is where the time goes. In his words, "I put a lot of your time into getting good at writing the content itself."

His actual daily routine is deliberately small. He posts, checks who viewed his profile in the last day, and sends a connection request to anyone who fits his ideal customer profile. Then he scrolls for ten or fifteen minutes and leaves comments only on posts he genuinely finds interesting, sometimes five, sometimes ten, sometimes none, because he finds it hollow to comment on content he doesn't care about. For outbound he sets a simple ceiling: "I would recommend sending 20 connection requests per day," which is roughly LinkedIn's limit and takes about ten minutes. The whole engagement block runs five to twenty minutes, then he logs off.

The point is a priority order, not laziness. Do the interesting work in your day as a founder, turn that into strong content, and only then spend whatever time remains on comments and connection requests. He concedes that beginners may benefit from light guardrails (say, five thoughtful comments a day) but warns that hours of scrolling and engaging hit diminishing returns fast. His summary is blunt: "You do not need to spend the entire day on LinkedIn to make this channel work."

## Lesson for Creators

If you only have an hour for LinkedIn, spend most of it writing, not commenting. Elaborate engagement pods and comment spreadsheets are largely engagement theater with a low ceiling. A workable daily minimum: post, scan your profile visitors and connect with those who fit your ICP, send up to ~20 connection requests, and leave a handful of genuine comments, all inside ten to twenty minutes. Then get back to your real work. Growth follows content quality far more than it follows time-on-platform.
