# The Bio That Works Like a Storefront

## The Story

Abby Rawlinson's growth advice for other creators starts not with content but with the profile itself. In her Instagram Growth Kit she describes the first stage of her method as fixing the foundation: "define your niche with clinical specificity, build a bio that works like a storefront, and develop a message that cuts through in a saturated space." The payoff she claims is a shift in when the account does its work: "Before anyone reads a single post, your profile will be doing the work."

The hard part of niching down is that specialists fear it closes doors. Abby's resolution is what she calls, in a [blog post](https://www.therapywithabby.co.uk/blog/therapist-niche-without-boxing-in), the Special Interest Approach. Instead of narrowing an entire practice, "you simply identify one area of special interest — something you feel particularly drawn to, curious about, or energised by." The account can still speak broadly; the *signal* is sharp. She reframes niche-finding as observation rather than invention: "Often, your niche isn't something you invent. It's something you notice."

Concretely, that special interest becomes explicit bio and listing language, e.g. naming a broad practice and then adding "a special interest in supporting late-diagnosed ADHD women navigating burnout." The specificity is a beacon, not a cage: "You're simply giving people a clearer signal about the kind of work you care deeply about." A visitor self-selects in seconds because the profile already told them exactly who it is for.

The mechanism is that the profile, not the feed, is the conversion surface. Reach delivers a stranger to the bio; the bio decides whether they follow. Abby front-loads the clarity there so that every post inherits an audience that already understands the promise, which is what let @therapywithabby compound to 218k rather than leaking cold traffic.

## Lesson for Creators

Optimize the profile before the posts. A visitor lands on your bio before they scroll, so make it a storefront: one sharp "special interest" stated plainly, so the right person recognizes themselves instantly. You don't have to amputate your range to niche, you only have to name the one thing you want to be known for. Find it by noticing which work already energizes you, not by inventing a category.
