# Rebrand Until the Pain Point Is Your Name

## The Story

The account that now reaches 350,000 people as *Anna the Anxiety Coach* did not start with that name, or that focus. Anna Ferguson trained in psychology at RMIT and spent about 13 years inside traditional mental-health systems before building her own practice online, a pivot she attributes on her site to frustration with how little relief those systems gave her own anxiety.

When she went out on her own, the positioning was broad. On the *Good Humans* podcast she traces the path. She started as *Optimal Health Co*, which by her description was all about gut health and mental health, a wide and undifferentiated wellness lane. She then rebranded to *Altered Flow*, reframing the work around helping people alter the flow of their lives and work with their bodies rather than against them. Only after that did the account become *Anna the Anxiety Coach*.

Each rename tightened the aperture. *Optimal Health Co* could be about almost anything; *Anna the Anxiety Coach* is about exactly one thing, and it puts the audience's search term, anxiety, directly into the identity. The final name is a positioning statement disguised as a handle: it tells a scrolling stranger in four words who this is for and what problem it solves, and it claims a specific niche instead of competing in the crowded wellness category.

The narrowing was not cosmetic. It coincided with the content that broke out, because a person searching for help with anxiety, or being shown anxiety content, immediately understood that this account was built for them.

## Lesson for Creators

A brand name is a filter. The broader it is, the harder your content has to work to explain who it is for; the narrower it is, the more the name does that job for you. Anna moved from *Optimal Health Co* (everything) to *Altered Flow* (a metaphor) to *Anna the Anxiety Coach* (one named problem), and only the last version put the audience's own word in the title.

If you are stuck at broad-wellness-style positioning, ask what single pain point you want to own and whether your name says it. Renaming to state the problem plainly is a low-cost move that makes every future post easier to find and easier to trust.
