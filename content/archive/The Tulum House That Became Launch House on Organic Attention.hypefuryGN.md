# The Tulum House That Became Launch House on Organic Attention

## The Story

Launch House was never meant to be a company. During the pandemic Houck wanted to be around other builders, so he "convinced 18 early founders to live together for a month" in a co-living house in Tulum. He and his future co-founder were planning to build something else entirely inside that house.

The attention arrived because they did it in public. As Houck told it, "we were living in Tulum during lockdowns, posting on Instagram and Twitter," and people noticed. The press picked it up, including the New York Times and TechCrunch, and strangers started asking how to join, when the next house was, whether they were incubating companies. The product they had planned was less interesting than the thing the posts had accidentally created.

So they followed the attention instead of their plan. "We zigged when others zagged," Houck said: when everyone else was isolating, they ran a founder house and broadcast it. They brought on a third co-founder, ran a second Tulum house with an application, then rented a place in LA where he personally interviewed 200 to 300 applicants that December and brought about 100 paying members into the community.

The momentum became the fundraising story. They had put over $200,000 of their own money in before any institutional check, and only kicked off a proper raise once the rising caliber of applicants and the public attention gave investors a reason to believe. The attention came first; the capital followed it.

## Lesson for Creators

You do not always know which project is the real one. Build in public and the audience will tell you, by what they ask for, where the demand actually is. Houck's company emerged from documenting an experiment on social, not from a plan. When organic attention shows up around something unplanned, the move is to lean into it, not to force the roadmap you started with.
