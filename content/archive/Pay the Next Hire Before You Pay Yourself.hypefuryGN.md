# Pay the Next Hire Before You Pay Yourself

## The Story

Lookout Media is profitable, but Geoff Sharpe has never taken a salary or a dividend from it. On the Creator Spotlight podcast (2025) he was candid that he even loaned the business money he has not paid himself back — while stressing the operation itself is genuinely profitable. Every dollar of that profit goes back into the product: _"I'm putting the profits back into the business."_ He can do this because his living comes from a separate agency, Sharp Strategies, which frees Lookout's cash to be spent on growth rather than on him.

Framed as a choice, it is stark. Given a surplus, the options are pay himself or hire another journalist, and _"I'm going to hire another journalist every single time."_ That is a deliberate inversion of the lifestyle-business model many local operators run, where the point is to keep the profit. For Geoff the point is to compound the product's quality by adding the people who make it.

The reinvestment is paired with hard risk discipline. He refuses to run the business in a deficit and only hires when he can cover roughly nine to twelve months of that role's cost without strain, using clear budget projections. He points to cautionary tales — The Messenger raised a fortune and shut down, and he questions how long fast-scaling players can sustain it — as exactly the failure mode his conservatism is designed to avoid. The journalists he hires expect a stable place to work for years, and he treats that as a trust he will not break by overextending.

## Lesson for Creators

When a bootstrapped media product turns a profit, the most aggressive growth move can be to not pay yourself. If you can fund your life another way, reinvesting profit into the people who improve the product compounds faster than pocketing it — but only under a hard rule against deficits. Expand a role only when you have already banked the runway to cover it for the better part of a year. Sustainability is not the opposite of ambition here; it is what lets the ambition survive a bad quarter.
