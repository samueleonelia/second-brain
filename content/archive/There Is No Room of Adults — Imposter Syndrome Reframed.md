# There Is No Room of Adults — Imposter Syndrome Reframed

## The Story

Matt's reframe of imposter syndrome doesn't rely on the usual "you belong here" reassurance. It rests on a more useful observation: nobody in the room you think you're an imposter in feels any different.

"Picture the highest levels of politics, or your career ladder, or whatever it is, you go into those rooms. They're having the same conversations. They're having the same doubts about their own feelings, their own decisions, and all this kind of stuff, and then they work through it, and they come to a decision. And that's it. They just go forward. That's the difference between the top and the bottom of the chain, is just the ability to continuously move forward" (Source 1).

He puts the same point in even blunter language on the Philip Wylie show: "There is no, like, you're never going to turn the corner and find the room of adults. Like, you're the adults now" (Source 2).

The advice the reframe produces is procedural rather than psychological: the senior people do not have a feeling you don't have, they have a behavior you don't have. They take the doubt, they make the decision anyway, they go forward. The differentiator is not confidence; it's the ability to continuously move forward despite the same doubts that paralyze everyone else.

## Lesson for Creators

The standard "you're not really an imposter" framing tries to remove the feeling. It usually fails because the feeling is genuine — the people one rung up actually do know more than you. Matt's reframe sidesteps the feeling entirely. The feeling is universal. The behavior — make the call, ship the thing, continue — is what separates levels. The actionable change is to stop treating the feeling as a signal that you should wait, and start treating it as the baseline noise everyone is operating through. The work is the same either way; the only question is whether you let the noise stop you. The advantage of this framing is that it gives you something to do today rather than a state to achieve someday.
