# Learning English From Tupac and Satellite TV

## The Story

Jasmin Alic grew up in Zenica, Bosnia, during the Bosnian War, which lasted four to five years. He had limited access to technology and resources as a child (Source 1).

English was not his native language. As a kid, he learned to speak English and German by watching satellite TV (Source 2). But the accent came from somewhere specific: American hip-hop. He picked up his English from listening to Tupac, Biggie, and Eminem (Source 1).

He went on to study English formally at university in Sarajevo and graduated with an English degree (Source 2). But the foundation was laid by rappers, not teachers.

That self-taught English became the foundation of his entire career. It led to teaching English in Bosnia and Saudi Arabia, then to copywriting on Upwork, then to building a global personal brand where he writes, coaches, and speaks professionally in his non-native language. His current clients include Microsoft, Digicel, Shell, and Marriott Bonvoy (Source 2).

## Lesson for Creators

The gap between Jasmin's starting conditions and his outcomes is staggering. A kid in wartime Bosnia, learning English from satellite TV and rap albums, eventually became the #1 ranked LinkedIn creator globally, writing in English for Fortune 500 companies. No formal English immersion, no native-speaking country, no early advantages. Just curiosity, satellite TV, and hip-hop. For non-native English speakers building online businesses, Jasmin is living proof that the language barrier is real but not terminal.
