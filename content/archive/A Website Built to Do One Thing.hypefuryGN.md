# A Website Built to Do One Thing

## The Story

Ask Eddie Shleyner how he built his email list and the answer is almost boringly direct: he asked, everywhere, for one thing only. "i built it going back to ctas by just asking people to subscribe like relentlessly," he says. The discipline is that the entire site is pointed at a single action. "my whole website is designed to make people subscribe to the newsletter," and, crucially, it does not compete with itself. A first-time reader is never pushed toward a product or a service.

He is explicit about the one-goal rule at the article level. "i have one goal on that on that article and that is to get you to subscribe or to go to another article which also has a cta to subscribe." Every path leads either to the signup or to more content that leads to the signup. He does not try to monetize a stranger on their first visit. He would rather get them onto the list, build a weekly relationship, and make any offer later, inside the newsletter itself.

The execution is not minimalist so much as monomaniacal. Growth In Reverse's teardown describes VeryGoodCopy's homepage carrying multiple subscribe buttons interleaved with heavy social proof (testimonials, awards, brand logos) and a subscribe button at the top and bottom of every article, plus free micro-courses as a signup incentive. The profile notes Eddie thinks about the whole website as one giant sales page whose only product is the newsletter. Many buttons, one goal.

That single-goal architecture is the conversion half of his growth machine. His borrowed reach and his LinkedIn loop drive traffic to the site. The site's job is to make sure that traffic does not bounce or scatter across competing offers, but converts into a subscriber he can reach again next week.

## Lesson for Creators

Decide the one action every visitor should take, then audit your site for anything that competes with it. Multiple calls to action are not a menu that helps the reader. They are a tax on your most important conversion. Point every page, every article top and bottom, and every path at the single outcome that matters (for most creators, the email signup), and resist the urge to sell a first-time visitor a product before you have earned a place in their inbox. Convert to owned first, monetize later.
