# Brand the Profile Before You Tell Anyone

## The Story

Chenell Basilio is a self-described introvert, and when she launched *Growth in Reverse* in December 2022 she was too nervous to announce it: "I didn't tell anyone I was doing this." Instead of a launch post, she did something quieter and more deliberate. She rewired her identity first: "I just like changed my Twitter profile to be all about growth and reverse." The profile was primed to sell the newsletter before a single person had been told it existed.

Then she went where her future readers already were and simply engaged. She started commenting on tweets, and, crucially, the comments were not pitches. As she put it, "that comment that I had was not about newsletter growth. It was just me being human and then people clicked through and they saw what I did" and subscribed. The play is a two-step: the comment earns the click through curiosity or goodwill, and the pre-branded profile does the converting.

The story that makes this concrete is Jay Clouse. Chenell had been in his community, and when she started commenting, one of the first people to comment back was Jay, who then clicked to her profile and subscribed, not realizing she had only just launched it. He was effectively her first subscriber. Later, around Christmas 2022, Jay mentioned *Growth in Reverse* in his own newsletter, and Chenell watched it move her "from like 20 subscribers to like 70," an early boost that felt enormous at that size. The relationship formed in a comment thread became a distribution event weeks later.

Her one tactical instruction folds the whole thing together: "make sure your whole profile is branded around what you're doing, what you're writing about, and then go engage with people." The comments create the surface area; the profile closes.

## Lesson for Creators

Most people flip the order: they launch loudly, then hope people check the profile. Chenell brands the profile first so that every stray click from an unrelated comment lands on a page already built to convert. The engagement doesn't have to be about your topic, and it works better when it isn't, because "being human" earns the click that a pitch would repel. For anyone cold-starting with no audience, the sequence is: fix the bio and profile to state exactly what you make, then go leave genuine comments in the rooms your future readers already occupy, and let curiosity do the rest.
