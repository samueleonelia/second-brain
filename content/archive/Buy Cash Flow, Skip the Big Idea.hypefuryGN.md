# Buy Cash Flow, Skip the Big Idea

## The Story

Dave Kline always knew he and his wife would run their own business; he just kept waiting for the big idea. He graduated with an engineering degree expecting to get a couple of years of business experience and then start something, but two years became ten, then twenty-plus, and the idea never arrived. The unlock was realizing he did not need an amazing idea at all: he was a reasonably good operator, and the world is full of lucrative, profitable businesses that just need to be run well. The reframe was to buy cash flow first: "what if we went and bought a business then we would have cash flow then we would have a Runway," and evolve the thing they bought into whatever came next.

They gave themselves a one-year runway to buy something, and used a year of exploring deals (from an oil-change franchise to various digital businesses) as a filter for what they actually wanted: a relatively high-margin, low-employee, portable business, because they had never done this and did not want to be beholden to a big payroll on day one. They ended up buying an education-review site that reviewed courses on platforms like LinkedIn Learning, Udemy, and Skillshare and earned a small commission when readers bought through those reviews.

The acquisition logic had two forward bets baked in. First, they saw cohort-based courses rising and thought they could grow the review business to cover those higher-ticket live courses. Second, they eventually wanted their own course content that the site could drive traffic to, so instead of a small affiliate commission they would capture the entire fee. Those two bets collided when they went through the Maven accelerator and tentatively offered a management course, expecting maybe eight or ten sign-ups just to offset the build cost. Per Kline, the first cohort drew a waitlist of about 150 people, and they taught ten cohorts in year one, five open on Maven and five run inside individual companies for their leadership teams.

## Lesson for Creators

You do not need a original idea to start; you need cash flow and a distribution asset you can steer. Buying (or building) something that already has an audience and revenue buys you runway and a warm channel, and the real product can emerge later by converting that audience from someone else's affiliate into your own higher-margin offer. Kline's review site was the on-ramp; the cohort course was the destination.
