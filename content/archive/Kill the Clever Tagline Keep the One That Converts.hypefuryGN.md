# Kill the Clever Tagline, Keep the One That Converts

## The Story

Maja was attached to a tagline for her launch. "My first tagline was from overwhelm to hyperfocus" and she pushed it hard, putting it on a webinar headline with Miro and into Meta ads.

The market disagreed with her taste. The clever line did not pull, and she paid to learn it: she ran it long enough to "lose a couple of thousands of euros because that bloody thing did not convert."

She replaced it with a blunt, literal promise, from strategy to profit, which then converted at 8.5% on her landing page. Her audience had no patience for vanilla cleverness, so the winning headline named the outcome directly. The point is not the words she chose but the judge she used: paid traffic and conversion rate, not her own preference or a branding expert's opinion.

## Lesson for Creators

Treat positioning as an experiment with a scoreboard. Put each candidate headline in front of paid traffic, read the conversion rate, and let a clever line die the moment a literal one outsells it.
