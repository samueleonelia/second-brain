# Written by Humans as the Editorial Tag

## The Story

On the day Mindstream launched in May 2023, Beehiiv hosted more than 1,000 AI newsletters. Superhuman and The Rundown were each past 500,000 subscribers. Sean Puri had publicly said six months earlier that someone needed to "do The Hustle for AI" and an army of operators had heard him. The category was saturated before Adam and Matt Village sat down in the garden shed.

What every competitor was doing was structurally identical. They were using AI to scrape the day's AI news, AI to summarize it, and Beehiiv's template to publish it. The newsletters looked the same, sounded the same, and read like a press-release feed. Adam's read of the gap was sharp: the absence in the category was not coverage, it was voice.

_"from day one, what we tried to do is not just report on the biggest AI news, but the AI news that we thought was really interesting and then to give an opinion on that news"_

Matt was a working copywriter at an agency. That mattered structurally, not stylistically. A real copywriter knows how to write a sentence that makes a reader feel something about a piece of news, which is the one thing AI-summarization tools in 2023 could not do. Every issue picked the news Matt found interesting, added a one-line journalistic angle ("here's what it means for you"), and signed off at the bottom with a single visible declaration.

_"And also we signed off every newsletter with written by humans"_

_"we had this excellent editorial team and talent that were um you know doing the job of surfing the web every day to find out what was going on and then adding summarizing it, curating it but adding some real editorial and journalistic insight"_

That sign-off was the cheapest piece of brand positioning in the company. It reframed everything above it: a reader who saw "written by humans" at the end of yesterday's issue knew, without being told, that the other 1,000 newsletters in their inbox were not. The competitive landscape stopped being a saturation problem and started being a contrast effect.

## Lesson for Creators

In a category where the dominant tool is the same one every competitor is using, the positioning move is not to find a smaller niche but to declare what your competitors quietly are. A working copywriter as your co-author is structurally different from a smart prompt, and saying so at the bottom of every issue lets the reader feel the difference without an essay about it.
