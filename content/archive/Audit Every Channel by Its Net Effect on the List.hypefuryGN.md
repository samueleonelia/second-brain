# Audit Every Channel by Its Net Effect on the List

## The Story

Most creators assume that more channels means more growth, so a podcast must help the newsletter. Aakash Gupta actually did the arithmetic and found the opposite. With a list around 170,000, every time he emailed the list about a new podcast episode he lost roughly 150 subscribers to unsubscribes, while promoting that same episode across social and other platforms won him back only about 10 free subscribers. Netted out, the podcast was shrinking his email list, not feeding it.

He does not flinch from naming that, even while he keeps the podcast for other reasons, and even while allowing it might partly be his own failure at distributing it. The discipline is that he refuses to let a channel coast on the assumption that activity equals growth. He looked at the one number that matters for the core asset — net change to the email list — and let it tell him a channel he is proud of is, on that specific metric, a drain.

The mechanism is honest per-channel accounting against the core asset, not gut feeling about which activities seem productive. Sending more email feels like marketing; for this channel the data showed it was attrition. Knowing that lets him decide what the podcast is for with eyes open, rather than telling himself a flattering story.

## Lesson for Creators

Measure each channel by its net effect on the asset you actually care about, and be willing to find that a beloved channel is costing you. The copyable move is simple subtraction: subscribers gained minus subscribers lost, per channel, per send. A channel that feels like growth can quietly be attrition, and only the net number will tell you.
