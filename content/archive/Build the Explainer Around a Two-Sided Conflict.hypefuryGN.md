# Build the Explainer Around a Two-Sided Conflict

## The Story

Asked how he makes heavy geopolitical and historical material readable, Pueyo points at conflict. On Infinite Loops he says that "conflict is something that people really really understand. It's the basis of storytelling." He then deliberately sets up the two sides and pushes them against each other through the piece.

The two-sided frame gives the reader stakes and a reason to keep reading through otherwise abstract material. It converts an explainer, which by default is just information, into a narrative with a question the reader wants resolved.

## Lesson for Creators

A fact list informs; a conflict pulls. Find the genuine two-sided tension inside your topic, two forces, two camps, or two outcomes, and build the piece on it. You never have to tell the reader to care, because the conflict does that for you.
