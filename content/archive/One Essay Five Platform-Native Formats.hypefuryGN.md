# One Essay Five Platform-Native Formats

## The Story

Growth In Reverse traced what happens after Kyla Scanlon hits publish on a Substack essay, and it is a schedule, not an impulse. The day the essay goes live, she posts an Instagram Reel that delivers a short-form version of the entire argument with a screenshot of the piece. The next day, a full YouTube video on the same idea. The same day as the video, the audio ships as a podcast episode. A few days later, the idea appears on LinkedIn as a text post, followed by the YouTube video shared there too.

Each format is rebuilt natively rather than cross-posted. The Reel compresses the essay to its stakes. The YouTube video expands it for an evergreen audience that finds videos months later. The podcast serves people who listen instead of watch. The stagger means one thinking session feeds five surfaces across a week, and each surface points people deeper into the ecosystem.

The traffic data shows why the breadth matters: SimilarWeb showed LinkedIn and Twitter driving far more website traffic than TikTok and Instagram, even though she posted on LinkedIn only about once a week. The platforms where she is loudest handle discovery, while the platforms where professionals share links handle conversion.

## Lesson for Creators

Repurposing fails when it means copy-pasting one asset everywhere on the same afternoon. Scanlon's version works because the formats are native and the rollout is staggered across days, which multiplies the surface area of a single idea without multiplying the thinking. The mechanical takeaway: put the rollout on a fixed day-by-day schedule per essay, and rebuild the idea per platform instead of linking to it.
