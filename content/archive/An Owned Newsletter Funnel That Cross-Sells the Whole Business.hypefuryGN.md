# An Owned Newsletter Funnel That Cross-Sells the Whole Business

## The Story

Two years after leaving his venture-backed company, Houck had turned $10,000 into a profitable business of roughly $1.2 million ARR that he owned outright. The structure underneath it was a stack: two newsletters, a membership, a marketplace SaaS, an agency, and a few smaller streams like founder advising. The newsletters are not the whole business; they are the mouth of the funnel that feeds it.

He was explicit about the architecture. The two newsletters, Founding Journey for founders and Viral Examples for people growing on social, "sit at the top of our owned-funnel." People enter by subscribing to one, whether through conversion ads, cold-emailed lead magnets that require an opt-in, organic posts, or referrals. Once they are on the list, the newsletter does two jobs at once.

The first job is direct sponsorship revenue. The second, which he stressed was equally important, is the chance to mention everything else he does without being intrusive. Founding Journey points readers to the membership and case-study library, to advising, to a fundraising support service, and to the content agency. Each issue is both a paid placement and a soft internal ad for the rest of the stack.

His warning was for creators who stop at collecting emails. "If you're building an email list but not using it to stay top of mind" for current and potential customers, he wrote, you are leaving a lot of revenue on the table. People are inundated daily, so you have to remind them you exist, often. The list is only an asset if it is actively pointing readers toward the things that make money.

## Lesson for Creators

A newsletter is worth more as a distribution channel for your whole business than as a standalone product. Put the list at the top of the funnel, monetize it directly with sponsorships, and use every issue to keep your other offers in front of readers who already trust you. An email list you collect but never sell into is potential revenue you chose not to collect.
