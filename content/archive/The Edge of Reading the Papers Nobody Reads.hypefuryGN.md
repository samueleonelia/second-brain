# The Edge of Reading the Papers Nobody Reads

## The Story

Pueyo's research input is primary scientific papers, not secondary commentary. On Infinite Loops he framed reading them as something rare: "I think that's one of the things that few people do," and added that "there's an edge in reading papers." On Summation he quantified the habit as "a dozen papers for each one of the articles that I write," across a lot of fields. The COVID essay was the extreme version of the same habit. He told Rebel Wisdom he had spent "two weeks researching this around for four to five hours a day" before publishing a word.

Because most writers stop at headlines and at each other's takes, going straight to the source material is an arbitrage. It produces denser, more defensible pieces that are hard for anyone skimming the same secondary sources to replicate.

## Lesson for Creators

The cheapest edge in a crowded niche is to read what your competitors will not. Go one layer deeper than everyone else, to the primary papers, filings, or raw data, and your output is automatically harder to copy and easier to trust.
