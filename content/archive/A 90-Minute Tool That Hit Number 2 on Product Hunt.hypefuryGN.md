# A 90-Minute Tool That Hit Number 2 on Product Hunt

## The Story

In December 2020 Jakob Greenfeld built What to Tweet, a page that shows a random tweet prompt with a real example tweet underneath, because he was struggling to stick to his own posting schedule. Total build: an Airtable holding the prompts and screenshots, a static Tailwind CSS page on Vercel's free tier, and a bit of JavaScript calling the Airtable API with a read-only key. He estimates all the steps fit in about 90 minutes. He was half expecting to be mocked for launching something so basic.

Instead it sat at #1 on Product Hunt for most of the day and finished as #2 Product of the Day with 490+ upvotes on Product Hunt, while makers who had spent months on their projects got almost no attention. His takeaway is unsentimental: "Product Hunt is not a fair competition in the sense that it doesn’t reward effort." What it rewards is simple tools many people instantly recognize they want, and if you personally use the thing regularly, odds are good others will too.

The growth payoff is the part most people miss: "Side-project marketing works. You can get a lot of attention with very simple tools." He is explicit about the transfer: "The launch helped me build my personal brand and generated a few sales of my completely unrelated products." The tool carried no ads at all, only the tiny made-by badge in the lower-right corner, the same badge that had earlier earned him a My First Million podcast shoutout. And he spells out who this mechanic is really built for: "Now imagine if I were the creator of a Twitter tool like Hypefury or Ilo". A free micro-tool adjacent to your paid product is a permanent top-of-funnel asset.

The stack is deliberately disposable: content lives in Airtable so updating the product means editing a spreadsheet, the frontend is copy-pasted Tailwind components, and hosting is free. Nothing about the mechanic depends on engineering skill; What to Tweet was later acquired, which he lists among his exited micro-projects.

## Lesson for Creators

A free tool is a piece of content that never stops circulating. When it solves a problem you demonstrably have yourself, it is pre-validated; when it takes hours instead of months, the downside of a flop is near zero and you can take many shots. Each launch buys attention that flows to whatever else your name is attached to, followers, subscribers and sales of unrelated products included. The discipline is keeping it small: spreadsheet backend, static page, ship the same week the idea arrives.
