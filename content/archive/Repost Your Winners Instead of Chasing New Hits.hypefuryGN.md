# Repost Your Winners Instead of Chasing New Hits

## The Story

The pressure most creators feel is to produce a fresh hit every single day, like a musician expected to drop a new chart-topper on every post. Tommy Clark rejects that. His view is that the content lifecycle on social is so short, and getting to a post that actually works takes so much effort, that once you have a winner the correct move is to keep using it.

Concretely, when a specific angle, story, hook format, or image format proves it performs, he runs it repeatedly: "just run that into the ground until it stops working." An overperforming post is not a one-time event to admire, it is a signal to do more of that exact thing. This saves the creative energy most people burn trying to reinvent themselves daily, and it leans into evidence instead of hope.

He takes it further with straight reposting. During a stretch when agency recruiting pulled him away from his own content, he leaned on his archive: "I'll go back 6 months, nine months, 12 months, and just find a post that did well, copy and paste it, post it today, no one notices, and oftentimes it'll do just as well, if not better." His two-part rationale: first, audiences are not watching closely enough to catch the repeat, so the social cost is essentially zero; second, if a post already proved it works, that is evidence it will work again. In a medium where content decays fast, he considers it almost foolish not to recycle what already won.

## Lesson for Creators

Keep a running list of your posts that overperformed, and mine it. When something works, don't retire it, extend it: reuse the hook format, the story, the image style until the performance actually drops. And when you're short on time or ideas, pull a winner from six to twelve months back and repost it close to verbatim. Almost no one will notice, and proven content tends to perform again. Treat your own archive as a reliable growth channel, not disposable history.
