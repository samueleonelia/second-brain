# Your First Build Becomes Both Content and Product

## The Story

Duncan Rogoff's first AI automation was not built to sell. It was built to solve his own LinkedIn problem: _"one of the first AI automations I ever built was an automation for LinkedIn. It would take trending news sources, it would write a LinkedIn post all in my tone of voice, and then it would publish it to LinkedIn."_

That single artifact then earned three separate times. It worked for him, it became proof-of-skill content, and it became his first paid work: _"This became one of my first YouTube videos, and this actually became one of the first client projects that I ever sold. I sold it for 600 bucks, and the rest is history."_ One build, three returns: tool, video, and deliverable.

The pattern compounds over time. The same trending-topic automation he first sold for $600 later became a free, installable skill that now anchors the top of his content funnel, the same idea repackaged as his audience and tooling matured. He treats every system he builds for himself as a candidate to be both taught and sold, which is why nothing he makes stays purely private.

## Lesson for Creators

Build for your own pain first. If it works for you, the build itself is content (show the wiring) and the result is a product (sell the outcome). One artifact can pay you as a tool you use, a video that grows an audience, and a client deliverable, so stop separating "what I use" from "what I sell." The first thing you make to scratch your own itch is usually your first sellable asset.
