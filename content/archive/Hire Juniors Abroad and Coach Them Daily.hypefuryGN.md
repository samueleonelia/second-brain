# Hire Juniors Abroad and Coach Them Daily

## The Story

Mindstream's first attempt at scaling beyond the two founders was the default route: a design agency, a paid-growth freelancer, an email-deliverability freelancer, plus contractors on cold-email outreach. Within a few months Adam concluded the model was structurally broken. The agencies and freelancers did adequate work and went home to their other clients. They were paid hourly or per-deliverable, and at those incentives nobody was going to care about Mindstream the way the founders did.

He broke the whole stack apart and rebuilt it with full-time juniors. The hiring channel was the LinkedIn audience he already owned. Adam wrote a deliberately informal callout, generated hundreds of inbound applications, and selected almost entirely from countries with strong English-speaking labor markets and unfavorable exchange rates.

_"we had employees from Lebanon, Pakistan, the Philippines, Iran"_

_"we were able to pay them like a very very good um rate for where they're from, which for us was more competitive than hiring um in in the UK or America"_

The hire was the easy part. The pattern that actually transferred operational judgment into them was unscalable on purpose. Formal monthly team meetings were tried, then dropped. In their place, Adam, Matt, and Matitz each ran daily one-on-one sessions with their direct reports, often 1-2 hours long, that did not stop after onboarding.

_"They are still doing that now like 18 months later. They are still spending a lot of time together"_

Adam framed the philosophy by citing MrBeast's doppelganger training, where employees live in MrBeast's house for three months specifically to clone his operational reflexes.

_"He literally has them come and live in his house and follow him around for 3 months so they can clone him"_

The transferred behavior compounded fast enough that Maria, the senior copywriter hired from Lebanon during her country's active war, was relocated by HubSpot to the UK after the acquisition and was operating with the same editorial instincts as Matt was. The "uncompetitive" structure of a small founder spending half a day in 1:1s with juniors turned out to be the only way to keep a small team's product judgment aligned at speed.

## Lesson for Creators

Juniors hired in underpriced labor markets are not a cost-saving move, they are an ownership-density move. The price you pay for that density is the founder time spent daily in 1:1s, which feels too expensive until you compare it to the cost of running a team that needs more headcount because nobody owns the judgment calls.
