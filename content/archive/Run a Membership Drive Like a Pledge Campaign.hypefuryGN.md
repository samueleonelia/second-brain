# Run a Membership Drive Like a Pledge Campaign

## The Story

Geoff Sharpe runs Lookout Media, a bootstrapped network of local-news newsletters in Canada (Ottawa Lookout, Van City Lookout, Capital Eats, Yukon Lookout) that reached roughly a $500K annual run rate with about 3,000 paying members, roughly 75% of revenue coming from memberships. A striking chunk of that money is not collected evenly across the year. On the Creator Spotlight podcast (2025) he said around 25% of annual revenue comes from a single December membership drive, a mechanic he lifted straight from how NPR, nonprofits, and charities have raised money for decades: _"campaigns as a mechanism for raising money work really"_ well.

The drive is not a discount blast. Geoff builds it as a month-long narrative. It opens with a concrete mission tied to real reporting — in one case a big development in Ottawa that readers were worried about, packaged as a crowdfunding push: fund this in-depth series by becoming a member. Then the story is fed in beats over the month: here is the issue, here is what we found, here are the writers who want to dig deeper, here is why we need the money, and here is how far short we still are. _"creating a story around what you're doing is incredibly powerful,"_ he said. The reader becomes a participant in the reporting rather than a passive donor.

Two levers do the heavy lifting: a target and a timeline. He frames it as a countable goal against a hard date — _"we need 30 new members to fund this series by October 31st"_ — and lets the deadline pull. _"setting that deadline helps anchor it in people's minds that something has to be done by this point."_ The result is heavily back-loaded: he sees roughly 30% of a drive's members arrive in the final week of the month-long campaign (Creator Spotlight, 2025). The December drive is also the year's biggest discount, but he runs the first half of it at full price and only introduces the discount partway through, so the price cut lands as an event rather than the whole pitch.

## Lesson for Creators

A recurring-revenue product does not have to sell itself the same way every week. Concentrate a slice of your annual ask into one campaign with three ingredients: a mission the audience can see the point of, a specific numeric target, and a real deadline. Give people a role in the story — funding a named piece of work — instead of a generic "support us." Then let the clock do the closing: expect most sign-ups in the final days, and design the arc so the last week has the most pressure.
