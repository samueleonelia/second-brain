# The Teacher Who Won 200 Medals Then Quit

## The Story

After graduating with an English degree from Sarajevo, Jasmin Alic worked as a teacher in primary and secondary schools in Bosnia and Herzegovina (Source 2). He also taught in Saudi Arabia, where he won the Educational Excellence Award in 2016 from Al Nasser School and the KSA Ministry of Education (Source 3).

His students won more than 200 medals in a single year, including gold medals in essays, debates, and public speaking competitions (Source 1). He had what he described as "a really good position" (Source 1).

Then he quit. He won an international teaching award in May and resigned in June (Source 1). His reasoning: "I understood that the education industry, the education world wasn't going anywhere" (Source 1). His teaching salary in Bosnia was 700 KM per month (Source 2), roughly equivalent to the $500-600 average salary in the country.

He chose freelance copywriting over a stable teaching career. The monthly salary of 700 KM was less than what he would later charge for a single hour of consultation.

## Lesson for Creators

Quitting at the top is counterintuitive but sometimes strategic. Jasmin didn't leave teaching because he was bad at it. He left because excelling at it showed him the ceiling. 200+ medals, international awards, and still $700/month. The gap between his output and his compensation was the signal. For creators weighing a career change, the question isn't "am I good at this?" It's "does being great at this get me where I want to go?"
