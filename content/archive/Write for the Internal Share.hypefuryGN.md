# Write for the Internal Share

## The Story

In May 2023 Aakash Gupta published a long onboarding guide that did unusually well, and when he traced where the new paid subscribers were coming from, the Growth In Reverse teardown of his business documents what he found: a product manager at Meta had read it and circulated it inside the company, and a cluster of paid signups followed from Meta staff. The insight underneath was that large tech companies carry learning-and-development budgets, so a $150-a-year subscription is trivially expensible for an employee who found the guide useful, and the only way to read the full thing was to become a paying subscriber.

So he stopped treating that as luck and started treating it as a target. He goes back through the pieces that drove the most paid subscriptions, re-reads them to understand why they spread, and writes more content built specifically to get passed around inside big organizations. The reader he is optimizing for is not just an individual; it is an individual embedded in a company where one share becomes ten, and where the budget to convert already exists. That turns a single well-placed reader into a node that distributes him internally and pays.

The mechanism is engineering content for intra-company circulation among audiences who can expense the product, which folds discovery and willingness-to-pay into the same act. It is the same compounding logic as his recommendation flywheel, aimed at the org chart instead of at other newsletters.

## Lesson for Creators

Identify readers who sit inside organizations that will both share your work and reimburse the price, then write deliberately for that internal pass-around. The copyable move is to audit which pieces drove paying customers, find the ones that spread inside companies, and make more of them. One reader who forwards you to a team with a training budget is worth far more than a reader who simply likes the post.
