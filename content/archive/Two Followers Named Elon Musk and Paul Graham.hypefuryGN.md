# Two Followers Named Elon Musk and Paul Graham

## The Story

Jakob Greenfeld ran the build-an-audience playbook on himself first. Starting out in entrepreneurship, he researched what kinds of tweets get attention, committed to at least two tweets a week and two blog posts a month, and even built a tool (What to Tweet) because he was struggling to stick to his own Twitter schedule. Looking back in his February 2021 essay *Build a business, not an audience*, he judged most of that time wasted, and saw the same trap catching many aspiring entrepreneurs: their goal is a business, but instead of building products they create content, which feels like progress precisely because it is risk-free.

His core claim is that "too many beginners have their priorities backwards". Content churned out to feed a schedule tends to be remixed platitudes, and that content selects its audience: it attracts fellow beginners, because everyone else recognizes it instantly. His thought experiment makes audience quality concrete: would you swap a 2,000-follower account built on feel-good platitudes for an account with two followers named Elon Musk and Paul Graham? In the Indie MBA conversation he sharpened it with a real case: "a friend actually who has a hundred thirty thousand followers now on Twitter makes zero dollars per month", still trying to figure out what the audience is for, because it was never built around a specific problem. The essay's verdict: "A high-quality audience is an endless source of opportunities. A low quality one is at most a Ponzi scheme."

The positive mechanism is publish-when-earned. Do meaningful work, solve real problems, and write only when you have first-hand insight that came out of that work, not to satisfy a self-imposed schedule. His internal quality test is emotional: "You should at least be a little scared before you hit the publish button." Fear signals you are saying something with intellectual risk, which is exactly what advances a conversation and gets the attention of the specific people worth attracting. And a hidden benefit: with a real story, writing skill largely stops mattering.

His entire craft advice fits in two lines: share meaningful first-hand experiences, and "Write as if you were emailing a friend, not to impress an imaginary teacher."

## Lesson for Creators

Follower counts are not interchangeable units; an audience is worth what its members can do for the problems you solve. Growth tactics that maximize raw count (remixes, platitudes, engagement bait) systematically fill the audience with people at the start of the road, which caps monetization at selling roadmaps. Inverting the priority (real work first, publishing as a byproduct of what the work taught you) grows slower in count but attracts operators, buyers and amplifiers, and the little fear before publishing is the cheapest quality gate available.
