# The Newsletter-Powered LinkedIn Growth Loop

## The Story

Eddie Shleyner runs a deliberate loop between the audience he owns (his email list) and the audience he rents (his LinkedIn following), and each turn of the loop makes the next one bigger. It starts with a single micro-article published on VeryGoodCopy.com. He repurposes that same article into a LinkedIn post, and describes the repurposing step in his own words: "i've been trying to compress them down for linkedin for social just like you know repurposing them." So the piece exists in two places at once, its owned home and its rented feed.

The clever part is the direction of the amplification. Per Growth In Reverse's teardown, he then emails the article to his newsletter with a call to action pointing subscribers to the parallel LinkedIn post (a button along the lines of "Know the feeling?"). When those subscribers like or comment on LinkedIn, the platform notifies their own connections, which exposes the post to thousands of second-degree readers who were never on his list. In the LinkedIn comments he links back to the newsletter signup, so a share of those new readers convert into subscribers.

That closes the loop: more subscribers this cycle means more people available to amplify the next post, which reaches more new people, who subscribe, and so on. Growth In Reverse reports the newsletter around 68,000 subscribers as of its December 2024 profile, with a LinkedIn following in the range of 110,000 to 119,000, and Eddie's own site states the list grows at roughly 100 subscribers a day. The owned asset feeds the rented one, and the rented one feeds the owned one.

What makes it durable is that it does not depend on any single post going viral. It converts the reliable, controllable action of an email send into distributed social reach, every issue, on demand.

## Lesson for Creators

If you have both an email list and a social account, do not treat them as two separate audiences. Wire them into a loop. Use the channel you control (email) to trigger engagement on the channel you don't (the feed), because most platforms reward early engagement by showing the post to the engagers' networks. Then capture those new arrivals back into the channel you control. The list is not just an output of your growth. It is the on-demand fuel that powers the reach that grows the list.
