# The Substack Recommendation Flywheel

## The Story

By the time Aakash Gupta had pushed Product Growth past 170,000 subscribers, the channel sending him the most new readers was not LinkedIn, where he had roughly a quarter of a million followers, and it was not his podcast. It was Substack's recommendation widget. As of mid-2025, more than 1,100 other newsletters were recommending Product Growth to their own readers. When someone subscribes to one of those newsletters, Substack asks whether they also want the newsletters that author recommends, and Product Growth sits on that list. That one mechanical hand-off, multiplied across 1,100 writers, became his largest single source of new subscribers.

The reason it compounds is that each recommendation is a standing asset, not a one-time spike. A writer who adds Product Growth to their recommendation list keeps sending a trickle of subscribers every time their own list grows, with no further effort from Aakash. The more writers recommend him, the more new writers discover him through those same readers, and the loop widens on its own. He is candid that this accumulated lock-in is now the main reason he stays on Substack at all, even though he thinks Beehiiv's feature set is better; walking away would mean abandoning 1,100 standing referrals.

The earned part sits upstream of the widget. Other writers only recommend Product Growth because they judged it good enough to put their own credibility behind. The recommendation network is the distribution layer, but the thing being distributed still has to clear the bar of a peer deciding it is worth sending to people who trust them.

## Lesson for Creators

A platform's native recommendation feature is the cheapest compounding distribution a newsletter can have, because each listing keeps working without you. The copyable action is concrete: get onto as many peer recommendation lists as possible, because this channel scales with the number of writers vouching for you, not with how often you post. The prerequisite is content other writers will stake their reputation on, which is why the lever only fires once the work is genuinely good.
