# The Personal Monopoly Is the Niche

## The Story

Aakash Gupta did not pick a niche by surveying what was popular. He picked the one place no competitor could follow him: the overlap of B2B growth, B2C consumer growth, and careers. Plenty of newsletters cover one of those well. Almost none cover all three, because almost no one has run growth across both business models and risen from PM to VP over a 16-year career. That overlap, he argues, is the only defensible place to plant a newsletter, and he calls it a _market of one_.

The framing was learned, not innate. In 2021 he paid $4,000 for David Perell's Write of Passage course, timing it to his paternity leave so he would actually use it, and the concept that stuck was the _personal monopoly_: write where your specific blend of experience makes you the only option. He tested it immediately, and the pieces that landed hardest were all inside that orbit, which solidified his belief that the intersection, not any single topic, was the strategy.

The mechanism is subtractive. Most creators widen their topic to reach more people and end up competing with everyone. Aakash narrowed to the exact coordinates only his resume could occupy, which removed competitors instead of adding readers, and let credibility compound on a claim no one could contest.

## Lesson for Creators

Find the intersection of two or three things your own history makes you uniquely credible on, and write there even though it feels narrower than the obvious niche. The copyable test is whether a rival could truthfully claim the same combination; if they can, go narrower until they cannot. A defensible position beats a broad one, because you cannot be out-competed on ground only you stand on.
