# Prove the Model by Relaunching It in a Harder Market

## The Story

Lookout Media started in Ottawa, a mid-sized city with a thin local-news market and few real competitors. That made Ottawa a weak test of whether the model was actually good or just early. So when Geoff Sharpe expanded, he did not pick another easy town — he went to Vancouver, a much more advanced news market with strong incumbent publications. On the SparkLoop podcast (2024) he framed Vancouver as a near make-or-break: _"if it works in a place like Vancouver then that tells us that the model works basically everywhere"_ — a place with lots of local news and a place without it.

The test was run like an experiment. Geoff tracks each publication's month-over-month metrics against where Ottawa sat at the same point in its own timeline: _"a lot of benchmarking to see how each publication is comparing where they are at each different spot of the timeline."_ Aligning the two cities by month-since-launch, not calendar date, let him ask a clean question: does a brand-new city hit the same numbers Ottawa did at the same age?

It did. _"we're basically hitting the same number of paying members the same same if not better sort of open rate"_ and click rate, he reported. That result did more than add a city. It converted a hunch into evidence that the editorial approach — not local luck — was what drove the outcome, which is exactly the proof he needed before committing resources to a third and fourth market.

## Lesson for Creators

Your first success might be the market, not the method. Before you scale a playbook, re-run it somewhere harder and instrument it: line the new venture's metrics up against the original's timeline, month for month, so you are comparing like with like. If a tougher market reproduces the numbers, you have evidence the model travels. If it does not, you learned that cheaply, before pouring money into replication.
