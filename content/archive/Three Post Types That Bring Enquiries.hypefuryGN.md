# Three Post Types That Bring Enquiries

## The Story

The middle stage of Abby Rawlinson's *Instagram Growth Kit* is not about posting more. It is a fixed menu of post types. She teaches that reach and enquiries come from a small, repeatable set of formats rather than from volume or novelty: "There are three post types that consistently bring therapists enquiries. I call them Magic Mirror, Real Talk, and North Star." Each archetype has a defined job, so a creator picks a slot instead of starting from a blank page every time.

Her stated principle is that the win is in selection, not quantity: "Content that lands doesn't come from posting more. It comes from posting the right things, in the right voice, for the right person." The three types are how she operationalizes that. Rather than chasing whatever might go viral, she rotates the same proven shapes and only swaps the topic underneath.

Her own feed shows the shapes in use. A June 2026 post opens by naming a hidden pattern in the reader, that what they call a business problem is really a nervous-system pattern, before offering the reframe. That is a recognizable Magic Mirror or Real Talk move: hold up a mirror, then reinterpret what the reader sees. Because the format is fixed, the audience learns what to expect and the creator spends their energy on the idea, not the structure.

The mechanism is that a named rotation turns content from a series of one-off bets into a system. Blank-page paralysis, the thing that quietly kills consistency, disappears when the next post is a choice among three known formats. The formats can be reused indefinitely; only the subject changes, which is what lets output stay both steady and on-brand across hundreds of posts.

## Lesson for Creators

Replace the open-ended question of what to post with a narrower one: which of my formats is this? Define a small set of named post types, three is plenty, each with a clear job (name a hidden pattern, share a real-talk take, point at the north-star belief). Then rotate them, swapping only the topic. Abby credits exactly this fixed menu for consistent enquiries, and her own reasoning is that landing content comes from posting the right things in the right voice, not from posting more.
