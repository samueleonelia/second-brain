# Share Everything for Free

## The Story

Growing up in Bosnia, where the average salary is $600, Jasmin's teaching salary was only $500 monthly. He couldn't afford educational courses and paid content (Source 2). That personal experience of being unable to access knowledge shaped his entire content philosophy.

"My LinkedIn strategy is 'share everything for free', and frankly, I don't regret it one bit." (Source 1).

"Sharing 100% doesn't mean you lose money. If anything, you have the advantage to earn more." (Source 2). He uses a skyscraper analogy: clients still need expert guidance for implementation even when they have access to all the information (Source 4).

Despite sharing everything, he earns through multiple channels (Source 3):
- 1:1 coaching: $5,000 for a 4-week program (90% booked going into the next year)
- Link Up community: 500+ members from different countries, $1,199/year or $1,999 lifetime
- Speaking, workshops, and corporate training
- Hey Jay agency: copywriting and brand strategy for Fortune 500 companies

He posts daily on LinkedIn to maintain visibility (Source 3). One idea gets repurposed into posts, lessons, workshops, and programs (Source 3). His approach: build trust before selling. "You don't need to sell in your content to be able to sell from your content" (Source 4).

The LinkedIn "deeper reason" post received 1,640 comments (Source 2).

## Lesson for Creators

The fear that free content cannibalizes paid offerings is common. Jasmin's model proves the opposite: free content is the top of a funnel that feeds coaching, community, speaking, and agency work. But his version isn't just a marketing tactic. It's rooted in personal experience of being unable to afford knowledge. That authenticity is why it works. People buy from someone who genuinely helped them before asking for anything.
