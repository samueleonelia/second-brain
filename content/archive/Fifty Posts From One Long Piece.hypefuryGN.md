# Fifty Posts From One Long Piece

## The Story

Erica Schneider spent four and a half years as head of content at the agency Grizzle, writing and editing long-form pieces. When she moved to social, she hit the context switch most long-form writers hit: a thousand words of evidence and long-winded story do not compress into a 3,000-character LinkedIn box or a 280-character tweet. She struggled at first, writing sentences that were too long and cramming in information nobody skimming had time for.

The system she built to solve it became her course Long to Short, whose promise is getting 50-plus social posts out of a single 1,000-word article. The first step is reverse outlining. Even though you know your own piece back to front, it is hard to see it structurally, so you go back through the draft and write one line for what each section said, either by hand or with a prompt. That immediately exposes the raw material, because as she puts it, "every single H2 or H3 point already can be its own post."

The multiplication comes from the second step: interrogating each point instead of just extracting it. She zooms in and zooms out on every section with a set of questions, the sharpest being "who's the enemy here." If the point is that content editing matters, the enemy is the person who says editing does not matter, just ship, which becomes its own post about the consequences of shipping unedited. She adds consequence framings on both sides: what happens to readers who ignore the point, and what better results come to those who follow it. Each of those answers spawns more posts, which is how one section becomes several and one article becomes dozens.

She runs the whole thing as an eight-step process precisely because a language model cannot handle it all at once, so each stage builds on the last: reverse outline, then face-value themes, then the zoomed-in and zoomed-out questions. She is emphatic that AI never writes the posts for her; it accelerates the outlining and question-asking, and she judges every output against her own strategy before anything ships.

## Lesson for Creators

One good long-form piece is not one post, it is a quarry. Reverse-outline it so each section stands alone, then multiply each section by asking who the enemy is and what the consequences are on both sides of the point. The interrogation, not the extraction, is what turns a single article into weeks of distinct social posts without repeating yourself.
