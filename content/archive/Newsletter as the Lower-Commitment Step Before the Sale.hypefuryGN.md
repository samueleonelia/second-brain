# Newsletter as the Lower-Commitment Step Before the Sale

## The Story

By 2023 Michael Houck had a decent following on X and LinkedIn, but he saw the risk in it. Newsfeed algorithms were about to get much better at deciding what people saw, which meant more distance between a creator and the followers he thought he owned. He called it the imminent TikTokification of every platform: reach you rented today could be throttled tomorrow.

His answer was to stop trying to sell directly on social, where an offer competes with an infinite scroll and loses. Instead he inserted a cheaper intermediate ask. Rather than push people straight from a feed to a checkout, "now there was an intermediate lower-commitment step you could offer them" — handing over an email address.

The mechanic is deliberately small at the front. As Houck put it, all you had to do was "give them a reason to share their email with you," and then remind them you exist each week by sending something valuable and building trust over time. The newsletter is not the product and not the sale. It is the holding pattern where a stranger becomes someone who recognizes your name in their inbox.

He noted that while email newsletters were hardly new in 2023, remarkably few founders and startups were setting up their funnels this way at the time. The sale still happens later, but it happens to people who have already opted in once and read for weeks, not to a cold feed scrolling past.

## Lesson for Creators

Social reach is borrowed and can be revoked by an algorithm change you do not control. An email list is the one audience you keep. Do not ask a cold follower to buy. Ask for the smaller thing first, the email, then earn the right to sell by showing up weekly with something useful. The newsletter is the low-commitment step that converts rented attention into an owned relationship.
