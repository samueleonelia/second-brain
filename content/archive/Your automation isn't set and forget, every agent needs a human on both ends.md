# Your automation isn't set and forget, every agent needs a human on both ends

## Idea
Agents don't run themselves: a human has to frame the task at the start and judge the output at the end, so the human is the "bread" on either side of the AI's work.

## Notes & Verified Quotes
Curation, relay-an-idea. Source: Dan Shipper (cofounder/CEO of Every), "After Automation." The framing image is Kieran Klaassen's "human sandwich."

The shape of the work:
> QUOTE: "we're the bread on either end of the AI's work" — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

> QUOTE: "a human sets the frame, AI collapses the task, and a human judges and extends the result" — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

Why "set and forget" fails — the agent's quality is tied to the human attached to it:
> QUOTE: "the agent needs a human in order for the work to, well, work" — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

Evidence it's not hypothetical: Every gave every employee a personal agent, then pulled back because unattended agents rot.
> QUOTE: "personal agents quickly get stale when the employees they were working with gave up on them" — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

My angle (to develop): "set it and forget it" is the selling line for most automation, and it's a trap. The win isn't an agent you forget; it's a workflow you can step into at the two points that matter (framing, judging) and ignore in the middle.
