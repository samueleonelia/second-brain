# Swap How-To Content for How-I Content

## The Story

As AI and ghostwriters flood LinkedIn with interchangeable advice, Tommy Clark says the single biggest shift in what performs is moving from *how-to* framing to *how-I* framing. The generic version titles a post something like *here's how to grow on LinkedIn*. The version that outperforms it is "here's how I've grown to 40,000 followers on LinkedIn" and the lessons picked up along the way.

The mechanism is not a wording trick, it is a sourcing constraint. A how-to post is advice anyone could write, so it reads as commodity and blends into the feed. A how-I post can only be written by the person who actually did the thing, which is why Tommy says the second variation is more compelling: "it comes from real world experience and nobody can copy that." The specificity (a real number, a real timeline, a real mistake) is simultaneously the hook and the moat.

This is the same logic that powers his Content Interview process: the reason to keep the founder in the loop is that their lived stories and opinions are the one input a competitor or an AI can't reproduce. How-I framing is that principle applied at the level of a single post. It forces the writer to anchor every claim to something they personally lived, which both raises credibility and starves the timeline's sea of generic advice of one more copy.

## Lesson for Creators

Before publishing an instructional post, check whether it could have been written by anyone in your niche. If yes, rewrite it in first person around a result you actually produced: replace *how to X* with *how I did X, and what it cost me*. Put the real number in the hook. You are not just teaching, you are proving, and proof from lived experience is the one thing nobody can lift from you.
