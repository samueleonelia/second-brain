# The Single Dad Driving Everything

## The Story

"Hi, I'm Jasmin. I'm 31. And I'm a single dad." That LinkedIn post received 1,128 comments (Source 1).

Jasmin grew up in Zenica, Bosnia, during the Bosnian War, which lasted four to five years. He had limited access to technology and resources during childhood (Source 2). At the time of his Forbes interview, he was living in his childhood bedroom in Bosnia (Source 2).

He described being a single dad as "his single biggest quality." Being a dad is why he works at all, why he goes "this hard," and his purpose for it all (Source 1). His son was almost four years old at the time of his podcast interview (Source 2).

When asked on the First Customer podcast what he would do if he couldn't fail, Jasmin answered: "I would just help people, like genuinely help people." He continued: "If you're not impacting others' lives, to hell with all of it" (Source 2). He prioritizes fatherhood over business growth, structuring his work around his son's schedule (Source 2).

At home in Bosnia, "nobody's got a clue about what I do" (Source 1).

## Lesson for Creators

Jasmin's story flips the typical "hustle" narrative. He's not grinding to scale a business empire. He's a single dad in Bosnia, working from his childhood bedroom, driven by the practical need to provide for his son and the philosophical need to help people. That clarity of purpose, being specific about why you're doing this, shapes every business decision. It's why he shares knowledge for free, why he stays in Bosnia despite global opportunities, and why he structures his business around flexibility rather than maximum revenue.
