# The Brand-Strategist's Unfair Start

## The Story

Before Abby Rawlinson was a therapist with 218k Instagram followers, she spent over 15 years inside marketing and brand-strategy teams for Burberry, Swarovski, and John Lewis (per her own [about page](https://www.therapywithabby.co.uk/about)). When she retrained and opened a private therapy practice, she did not start as a blank-slate creator learning positioning by trial. She arrived already fluent in it.

She is direct about the head start this gave her. On her page for other therapists she writes: "Before I retrained as a therapist, I worked in marketing for over 15 years. That experience helped me position and fill my private practice almost instantly." The account @therapywithabby was, in effect, a brand launch run by someone who had launched brands for a living. Positioning, messaging, and audience came first; the therapy credential was the product underneath.

What makes this a growth mechanism rather than a lucky biographical detail is what it replaced. Most creators spend their first years discovering, the slow way, what a professional marketer already knows: who the audience is, what single promise the profile makes, and how to say it so it cuts through. Abby skipped that discovery phase because she had run it hundreds of times for large brands. The audience compounded from a positioned starting line instead of a cold one.

The transferable point is not to have had a marketing career. It is that the growth work happens *before* the first post, and it is a discipline you can borrow. Treating a niche account as a brand launch (defining the audience, the promise, and the message deliberately) is what turned a new therapist's page into a platform that later carried a Penguin book deal, courses, and a 15,000-subscriber newsletter.

## Lesson for Creators

The biggest lever on a new account is applied before you post: who exactly it is for, and the one promise it makes. Abby didn't grow fast because she found a content trick; she grew fast because she treated the account like a brand she was positioning for a client. You can do the positioning work deliberately (audience, promise, message) instead of waiting years to stumble into it.
