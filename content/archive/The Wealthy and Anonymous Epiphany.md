# The Wealthy and Anonymous Epiphany

## The Story

For the first seven years of his entrepreneurial career, Yong-Soo deliberately stayed offline. "I never considered sharing any thoughts, really. At the end of the day, I wanted to be wealthy & anonymous. I had no intention of sharing anything because I was concerned that being public about my wealth might put a target on my back" (Source 1).

Then the calculation flipped. "But one day, I had a huge epiphany. I realized that sharing my journey online would actually speed up my process and get me closer to my goals. The upside was way higher than the downside risk" (Source 1).

The reasoning was a market gap, not a content urge. "I also realized that not many operators who are in the trenches of building an 8-figure business are online talking about building things. This put me at a huge advantage as someone who is an actual operator building companies" (Source 1). The First Class Founders podcast launched in November 2022, eight years after his first business.

The choice came with consequences he didn't initially anticipate. By the time he gave the Bootstrapped Founder interview, he was describing a side effect of personal branding he hadn't priced in: "I'm already thinking about the hook for a post on X. So I'll be like, oh, I'm going to do this thing right now. This is going to be amazing story. I can't wait to write about it. I already have the hook in my head" (Source 2). His wife noticed the shift: "she's like, why does that matter? Like, what can't you just like, think from a, you know, like just decide based on how you feel and what you think is the best instead of thinking about the audience and how they would react to your story. I was like, oh, my God, you're right. Like, I'm changing, like I'm changing in a way, maybe I don't want to change" (Source 2).

He doesn't recommend the path universally: "I do think there's something to be said about an operator who is just really good at building a company and but don't care to be scrutinized by the public. And they are just building something quietly, but it's killing it because they know all the growth channels to hit and they're getting customers" (Source 2). "It's not for everybody" (Source 2).

## Lesson for Creators

Most creator-business advice assumes you build the audience first and the business follows. Yong-Soo inverted that — eight years of operating before a single tweet — and his pitch to himself for going public was specifically that the conversation about operating was dominated by people who hadn't operated. That's a defensible positioning thesis, not a vanity move. But the second half of the story matters too: once he started optimizing for hooks, the inputs to his decisions started warping toward "what makes a good post." If you cross from operator into creator, expect that re-wiring and decide ahead of time which decisions you'll deliberately make off-camera. The advantage of being an actual operator only holds as long as you're actually operating.
