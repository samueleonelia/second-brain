# The Comment-to-DM Funnel

## The Story

Abby Rawlinson repeatedly ends posts with a single instruction: comment one word to receive something. Her June 2026 masterclass post closes, "Comment HOLD and I'll send you the details." The same move recurs across her feed with different trigger words tuned to the offer, e.g. commenting a keyword to be sent a free article, and commenting a keyword to be sent a link to a paid £59 audio course. One structure, many payloads.

The mechanic does three jobs at once, which is why it beats a plain link-in-bio. First, it manufactures comment volume: asking for a one-word comment is a near-zero-effort action, and comments are among the strongest signals Instagram's ranking rewards, so the ask itself amplifies the post's reach. Second, it opens a direct-message thread, moving the relationship from a public feed into a one-to-one channel where attention is far higher. Third, and most important for compounding, it captures an owned-audience contact: the DM is where the free article or course link is delivered, which is the on-ramp to her 15,000-subscriber newsletter and her courses.

The elegance is that the reader believes they are simply asking for a resource, while the creator is running acquisition. The "cost" to the follower (type one word) is trivially low, so conversion is high; the value to Abby (reach signal + DM + a step toward an owned contact) is large. She is not renting the audience from the algorithm and hoping they click a bio link later; she is pulling them, post by post, toward a channel she controls.

Because the same skeleton works for a free lead magnet or a paid product, the funnel scales without new infrastructure: pick the offer, pick the keyword, append the line. It turns ordinary content into a repeatable capture event.

## Lesson for Creators

Give every post a job beyond the like. A one-word call to action (*comment WORD and I'll send you the thing*) is low-friction that simultaneously boosts reach (comments are a ranking signal), opens a high-intent DM, and moves a stranger toward an audience you actually own (email, a course). Keep the ask to a single word so the barrier is near zero, and vary only the offer and the keyword, not the structure.
