# Brand 30 Daily-Posting Challenge as a Distribution Engine

## The Story

Andy Mewborn was early to LinkedIn content, alongside Justin Welsh, Kevin Dorsey and Scott Leese, while still at Outreach (he recalls the crew on the *Colin Cadmus* podcast). Rather than only posting himself, he packaged the habit into a product: Brand 30, a 30-day personal-brand challenge that pushes participants to publish consistently and stop fearing the publish button. On the *Bridge the Gap* podcast the host describes it as a roughly 4,000-member LinkedIn group that helped him and his wife build and scale their LinkedIn presence.

The mechanism is a distribution engine, not just a course. Every participant who starts posting is a new node reintroducing Andy's frameworks to their own network, so the challenge compounds his authority while it grows theirs. Andy later rebranded Brand 30 into what he calls the go-to-market Elite Circle, and, crucially, he engineered it to be sticky. As he explains, the biggest risk to a low-ticket SaaS is churn, so he bundled the paid community with the software: at roughly 99 dollars a month you get the tool and the circle. The community is the retention hook — "people don't want to leave the community even if they're not using the SAS."

Andy's own newsletter (andysnewsletter.com, which he pegs at 31,000-plus subscribers) runs Brand 30 as recurring cohorts with weekly modules — a buyer-first content module, a one-pager system, and so on — and leans on participant proof to fill seats. In one issue he cites a participant, Timothy, booking about 850,000 dollars in pipeline in 7 days off a single post that reached a CRO with no prior relationship (andysnewsletter.com; participant outcome he reports, not Andy's own number). Visible wins from members become the marketing that pulls the next cohort in.

## Lesson for Creators

You don't have to be the only one posting to grow. Turn your method into a time-boxed, public challenge (30 days, one post a day) and every participant becomes a distributor of your ideas — their reps build your authority while building theirs. Then make the community itself the product people won't cancel: bundle it with whatever you sell so the belonging, not the feature list, is what retains them.
