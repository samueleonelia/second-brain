# Content Market Fit Before Any Demo

## The Story

When Adam Robinson started posting on LinkedIn in January 2022, it was for Retention.com, not RB2B. Retention.com was at roughly $13M ARR with six people (his account, Ahrefs Podcast), and he had just decided that big Shopify stores were the dream customer. The plan was to build a personal profile to make that audience aware of the brand. It did not work, at least not the way he expected.

For about the first year, the content was not producing visible business. It booked no demos. What made it hard, in his telling, was that he could not explain why it was worth doing. He borrows a line from internet marketer Russell Brunson to describe the state: posting on social in the early days is not about deals or dollars, it is about being _"one day closer to finding your voice."_ Adam is blunt that this is uncomfortable. He says _"it didn't really work for like nine or 12 months and then something clicked"_ (ProducTea with Leah).

His frame for the struggle is a deliberate parallel to product-market fit. Just as a startup can spend years chasing PMF, he argues _"there is a very real content market fit that you need to find and it is not easy"_ (Ahrefs Podcast), and you know you do not have it because you are posting into nothing. What kept him going was that the content was quietly doing other things: he went from unknown in the e-commerce ecosystem to being recognized at trade shows, and it helped recruiting, partnerships, and general credibility even while it drove zero demos. Those top-of-funnel signals were the evidence that the channel was alive before the bottom-of-funnel numbers moved.

The payoff came later and in a different market. When he re-pointed the same account at B2B revenue leaders around Labor Day 2022 and then launched RB2B on March 1, he already had an engaged audience built over six-plus months. The voice was found; the demos followed.

## Lesson for Creators

The trap is measuring a top-of-funnel activity with a bottom-of-funnel ruler. A founder audience takes months to convert, and Adam's own clients (per the ProducTea discussion) abort the experiment right before it works because they stare at signups instead of reach. Treat the first 9-12 months as voice-finding, not lead-gen: judge it on whether people start recognizing you, not on demos booked. Content-market-fit is a real, separate milestone, and it usually arrives before the sales does.
