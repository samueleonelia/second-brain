# A 52-Page Voice Doc That Lets AI Draft in Your Voice

## The Story

Katelyn Bourgoin is not a fast writer. She says a single LinkedIn post could take her 90 minutes to three hours, and the newsletter could eat two-thirds of a day. Rather than accept that as the ceiling on her output, she built a tool. Over roughly 100 hours she assembled a content co-writer inside Claude, "trained on my brand strategy, my voice, my posts that have been successful." It is not a clever prompt. It is an instruction set: "the instructional doc is 52 pages long," backed by several more framework documents.

The internals are the interesting part. There is an authority science framework covering four content types (problem, point of view, promise, proof) with dozens of sub-frameworks underneath. There is a document on writing hooks from principles rather than swipe-file templates. There is a voice profile trained on her own past content. Critically, the co-writer does not autocomplete generic posts; it interviews her. She drops in an idea by voice note on her morning walk, and the tool asks her questions about her perspective and her own experiences, then pulls proof points and stories from an archive of her business to draft something that reads like her — because the raw thinking is still hers. The result: a post that once took 90 minutes to three hours now takes closer to 45.

Her thesis on why this beats one-line magic prompts is a growth argument. In an AI world, everyone can produce work that is merely okay, and okay is no longer enough. The creators who get real leverage are the ones who invest the hours to codify their craft and intuition into the tool, so the output clears the bar that generic AI never will. The 52 pages are the moat.

## Lesson for Creators

If writing is your bottleneck, do not reach for a short prompt and settle for average output. Do the opposite: over-invest once in encoding how you actually think — your strategy, your frameworks, your hook principles, your voice, your best past work — into a long, specific instruction set. Then let the AI interview you rather than replace you, feeding it your raw ideas and stored stories so the draft stays yours. The hours you spend building the system are what keep your AI-assisted output from sounding like everyone else's.
