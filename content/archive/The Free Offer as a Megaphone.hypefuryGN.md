# The Free Offer as a Megaphone

## The Story

RB2B launched with a deliberately extreme free tier: it resolves anonymous website visitors to a LinkedIn profile and business email and pushes them to Slack, free and unlimited for a month, and free forever for roughly 85-90% of accounts, with only the top slice paying. Adam Robinson's reasoning is that the free offer and the audience are the same engine. He had spent months building an engaged LinkedIn following before launch, and he reasoned that a massive free offer _"is going to work way better for me with this megaphone than anybody else who didn't have it"_ (ProducTea with Leah). The order of operations mattered: _"the distribution was informing the go to market decisions"_ (ProducTea with Leah), rather than the product deciding the marketing.

His template was MailChimp, which he watched beat the public incumbent Constant Contact by making 95% of that incumbent's customers free, and bootstrapped its way to a $12B exit. Adam is trying to run the same play in contact and intent data, giving away for free what companies like ZoomInfo or Apollo charge tens of thousands for, betting that a light, self-serve, low-churn point solution can become synonymous with the category, and with his face.

The distribution mechanic is that free users are not a cost, they are a growth channel. He cites a friend's principle that _"no user is a free user"_ (ProducTea with Leah): every user gives you something, whether reviews, SEO signal, or, most valuably, referrals, because a free user knows other people who will pay. Early data backed the virality; Adam says _"I can tell just by the metrics that the free offer is viral on its own"_ (ProducTea with Leah), with the majority of signups self-installing the tracking script with no salesperson involved. People evangelize it precisely because it feels like getting away with something: one of his own team members said he couldn't stop referring people to it.

## Lesson for Creators

A free offer and an audience multiply each other. If you have already built distribution, a genuinely generous free product spreads through it far faster than through paid channels, and the free users become your referral and word-of-mouth layer rather than a drain. Let what you can already distribute shape the go-to-market: sometimes the strongest move is to give away the thing everyone else sells.
