# Newsletter as Pipeline Bait, Then Follow the Signal

## The Story

After her VC-backed startup went bankrupt, Katelyn Bourgoin took marketing work with her former investor's portfolio companies and kept hitting the same wall: brilliant technical founders who could not answer a simple question about who their customer actually was. She decided the real opportunity was a productized customer-research agency for B2B SaaS companies. The newsletter was never meant to be the business. It was bait. She started it to build pipeline for the research agency, and she picked its topic by reverse-engineering the job her prospects were trying to do: understand why people buy. So the newsletter became a stream of behavioral science and buyer psychology.

The agency thesis was wrong. B2B SaaS companies did not want to buy customer research — but, as she put it, "they did care about my little newsletter that I started off as a pipeline builder for my research agency, which was all about behavioral science and marketing and buyer psychology." The lead magnet outgrew the thing it was supposed to feed. Rather than force the agency, she followed the signal into the topic the audience actually rewarded, and "I became known as like the buyer psychology person." That became her first ownable idea.

*Why We Buy* compounded from there. Per the Kit case study she detailed, the list was around 20,000 subscribers when she moved onto Kit and grew to close to 60,000 email subscribers; the *Growth In Reverse* teardown puts her total cross-platform audience around 220,000. The point is not the numbers. It is that the durable business was hiding inside the funnel she built to sell something else, and she noticed.

## Lesson for Creators

Your top-of-funnel asset is a live experiment, not just a pipeline. Pick its topic by the job your ideal buyer is already trying to do, publish, and then watch which thing people actually pull toward. If the side-project newsletter is what earns attention while the offer you meant to sell stalls, that is the market telling you where the demand is. The move is to follow the signal and rebuild the business around what the audience rewards, instead of dragging them toward the offer you planned.
