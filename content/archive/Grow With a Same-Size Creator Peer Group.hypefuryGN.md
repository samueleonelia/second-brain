# Grow With a Same-Size Creator Peer Group

## The Story

Asked for tactics founders could use to build a brand they could carry with them, Houck pointed at the people one rung over, not the megastars. His advice: "Form a peer group with creators in your niche and similar size" and grow together. He cited MrBeast doing exactly this early, studying the craft alongside other creators rather than in isolation.

The entry move is engagement, not a pitch. Be authentic and "comment early on their posts so they recognize you before you DM." By the time you reach out, you are not a cold stranger asking for a cross-promo; you are the person who has been showing up in their replies for weeks. The relationship is already warm because you built it in public, one comment at a time.

That warmth is where the actual distribution happens. Houck noted that collaborations and recommendations with peers matter a lot, and that "a lot happens in DMs and group chats" out of public view. Same-size peers cross-promote each other because the exchange is even: each side brings a comparable audience, so neither is doing the other a favor.

He was candid that the startup niche is a hard room, with a discerning, time-strapped audience, but said credibility plus visible results make it a strong place to build if you can show you have done the thing.

## Lesson for Creators

Do not chase creators ten times your size for a shoutout; build a cohort at your own level and rise together. Earn recognition before you ask by commenting early and often, so the eventual DM lands as a familiar name. Peer cross-promotion compounds because every member is growing the others' audiences at the same time.
