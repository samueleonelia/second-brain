# Five Reels a Day for Six Months

## The Story

Before Anna Ferguson became *Anna the Anxiety Coach* to a 350,000-follower Instagram audience, she spent roughly half a year publishing at a pace most creators never attempt. In a long interview on the *Good Humans* podcast with Cooper Chapman, she described the grind plainly: she had been posting reels at a frantic pace, sometimes four or five a day, for almost six months straight.

The output did not produce overnight scale. After all that volume she had grown to only about 5,000 Instagram followers, a number that would discourage most people who had shipped hundreds of videos. But the point of the cadence was not any single reel; it was the accumulation of attempts. Each reel was another test of a hook, a claim, and a framing, on an audience she was still learning to reach.

Then, around the six-month mark, the account behavior changed. As she put it, things just started to shoot off. The reps had done two things at once: they had trained her at making the format, and they had given the algorithm a large body of work to eventually surface. The breakout reel (covered in a separate card) did not come from a standing start; it came after months of daily volume had built the muscle and the back catalog.

The mechanism here is quantity as a discovery engine. Anna did not know in advance which message would land, so she manufactured as many chances as she could, then kept going long enough for one to hit.

## Lesson for Creators

Treat early posting as at-bats, not as a portfolio. If you do not yet know which of your ideas will resonate, the fastest way to find out is to raise your volume and shorten the feedback loop, not to polish one post for a week. Anna shipped four or five reels a day for months and sat at 5,000 followers before anything broke; the willingness to keep going through a flat stretch is what put her in position to catch the winner when it came.

A flat six months at high volume is not evidence the strategy is failing. It is often the cost of buying enough attempts for the algorithm and your audience to eventually pick a winner for you.
