# Everyone thinks automation means less work, the teams automating fastest have more of it

## Idea
The more you automate, the more expert human work you create, because AI commoditizes yesterday's competence and pushes all the remaining value onto the judgment, maintenance, and framing only a human can supply.

## Notes & Verified Quotes
Curation, relay-an-idea. Source: Dan Shipper (cofounder/CEO of Every), "After Automation."

The paradox: Every automated everything they could (Codex, Claude Code across coding, writing, design, support) and still have ~30 people and more work than ever.

> QUOTE: "the more we automate, the more expert human work there is to do" — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

The mechanism (why automation doesn't subtract work):
> QUOTE: "Making expert work cheaper does not simply replace experts. It creates more situations where expert judgment is needed." — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

First-order reason: agents themselves are work. They need constant maintenance, and the "simple" automations aren't simple.
> QUOTE: "One of our PowerPoint automations includes 24 skills and 18 scripts and costs $62 in tokens to make a single deck." — src: scratch/sources/after-automation.txt | source: https://every.to/p/after-automation

My angle (to develop): the pitch for automation is "buy back your time," but in practice it relocates your time — from doing the task to running the system that does the task. That's not a bug; it's the job changing shape.
