# The Box Got Bigger but Remained a Box

## The Story

Jakob Greenfeld launched his newsletter with the tightest positioning imaginable: it was literally called Product Ideas and covered only product ideas for indie entrepreneurs. Readers liked it and it was growing, but within months the niche focus started grinding against his identity as a writer. He kept having ideas that did not fit the name, and a niched-down name is a promise about what you will and will not publish.

So he rebranded, twice, in baby steps. First to Opportunities.so, which allowed opportunities in a broader sense. Then to Founder Flywheel, meant to allow any founder-relevant content. His verdict on the whole ladder, from the July 2022 essay: "The box got gradually bigger, but it always remained a box." And the conclusion he draws for anyone starting today: "If I could start all over again, I would definitely use a brandable name instead of a niched-down name." He points at James Clear, who according to Jakob grew a newsletter past 100k subscribers without giving it a name at all.

The essay generalizes the pattern from studying creators like Tim Ferriss, James Altucher, Nat Eliason and Seth Godin: almost none of them started with a niche. They wrote about whatever held their curiosity, watched what resonated, then doubled down. "This is how they found writer-content-audience fit." The niche is the output of the experiments, not the input. Jakob is precise about when specificity does help: when testing individual hypotheses (write several posts on one topic and distribute them where the target readers are), and when switching from exploration to exploitation. "A tight positioning is like rocket fuel." It accelerates a fire that already exists and smothers one that does not.

There is also a defensive argument. People follow people, not content machines. A reader who is angry that you changed topics was treating you as a commodity, and commodity newsletters are replaceable the moment they pause. Building under your own name makes you the only supplier of you.

## Lesson for Creators

Premature niching trades your creativity, the one input everything depends on, for a label your ego likes. Run topic experiments under one umbrella brand (ideally your name) so each pivot keeps the audience and the SEO you already earned, and adopt a tight positioning only after the data tells you which topic deserves it. Naming the niche too early means paying for it later in rebrands, or in a box you quietly resent.
