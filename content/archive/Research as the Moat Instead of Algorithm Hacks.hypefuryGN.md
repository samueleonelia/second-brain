# Research as the Moat Instead of Algorithm Hacks

## The Story

The standard LinkedIn growth advice is tactical: spend twenty minutes a day commenting on big accounts, jump on every trend, ship carousels, farm engagement. Katelyn Bourgoin grew to roughly six figures of LinkedIn followers while largely refusing all of it. By her own account she never really jumped on trends and "I probably published like five carousels." She admits she does not play the comment game — she does not have the bandwidth or attention for it — and concedes she might have grown faster if she had.

What she did instead was lean on an asset the tactics crowd does not have: research. She runs a newsletter about why people buy and builds products on the psychology of copywriting, so she is constantly studying what actually makes a human pause, pay attention, and act. She then applies that same research to her own posts. As she describes her edge, her shortcut is "geeking out about human behavior and I've never really chased algorithm." The persuasion knowledge is the moat; the algorithm is a rented tactic.

The tradeoff is deliberate. She says plainly that "I kind of ignore a lot of the more trendy stuff" and focuses instead on understanding who she serves and what kind of writing will attract them. That means the content itself carries the growth, rather than a distribution hack that stops working the moment the platform changes the rules. Trend-chasing has a short shelf life; understanding why people buy does not.

## Lesson for Creators

Engagement hacks decay — the comment trick or the trendy format that works this quarter is gone the next. Durable understanding of your audience does not. If you have to choose where to invest, choose the research: study what genuinely persuades the people you want to reach, and put that into the substance of every post. You can grow slower than the pod-and-carousel crowd and still build something that keeps working after their tactic dies.
