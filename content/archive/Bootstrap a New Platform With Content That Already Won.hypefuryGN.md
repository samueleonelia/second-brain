# Bootstrap a New Platform With Content That Already Won

## The Story

Katelyn Bourgoin did not build her X and LinkedIn audiences in parallel. She built X first, committing to it around 2020, and treated LinkedIn as an afterthought until roughly late 2023 into 2024. That sequencing is the whole mechanism. By the time she got serious about LinkedIn, she was not staring at a blank page wondering what would land. She already had years of posts with a track record.

So she started on LinkedIn by "reposting the stuff that worked well on X." Concretely, she was taking the threads that had already earned engagement and "turning those into LinkedIn posts." The winners were pre-selected by the first platform's audience; LinkedIn just got the greatest-hits version. Over time the center of gravity flipped, and she came to prefer LinkedIn, where she reached roughly 92,000 followers (as stated in the *Connection Accepted* interview) — a distinct metric from her separate X following and from her *Why We Buy* email subscribers.

The reason this is a growth lever and not just a time-saver: new-platform reach is expensive because you are guessing. Republishing proven content removes the guessing. Each repost is an idea that already cleared the bar with a real audience, so the hit rate on day one of the new platform looks like the hit rate you earned after years on the old one.

## Lesson for Creators

Do not treat a second platform as a fresh start that resets your batting average. Treat your first platform as an R&D lab that has already told you which ideas work. When you expand, port your proven posts first — the threads, hooks, and angles that already won — rather than inventing net-new material for an audience you do not understand yet. You get to skip the cold-start guessing phase because someone else's feed already ran the experiment for you.
