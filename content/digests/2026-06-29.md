# Digest 2026-06-29

Six new notes, all distilled from one source: Goku Mohandas's "Stop Renting Your Intelligence — a technical guide to building your own learning loop."

## Key ideas

- The moat is the loop, not the model. A frontier model is a commodity input two competitors can rent identically; the durable asset is the closed feedback cycle wrapped around it (your evals, RL environment, post-training on an open base you control) so your data and usage compound into IP nobody can rent back. Credited to Satya Nadella's "human capital and token capital compound" framing. [[The Moat Is the Learning Loop Not the Model]]
- Renting leaks your institutional knowledge. Every prompt, trace, and especially every embedded forward-deployed-engineer engagement is a one-way transfer of signal: you fund the lab building its loop against yours, then keep renting a model trained on your own work. [[Using a Frontier Model Leaks Your Institutional Knowledge]]
- What a learning loop actually is. An RL environment is a programmable simulator of your business (state, action, transition, reward) whose sandbox is the proprietary part no API hands you; and RL beats fine-tuning because it learns to achieve outcomes and regenerates fresh training signal from every rollout, where a fine-tune is a snapshot that decays the moment your data shifts. [[An RL Environment Is a Programmable Simulator of Your Business]] [[RL Learns Outcomes While Fine-Tuning Imitates Labels]]
- Hosting is table stakes; extending is the moat, and you start with embeddings. OSS runtimes (Ray, vLLM, SGLang) and open models (Qwen, Llama, DeepSeek) have made hosting a solved problem, rebutting Amodei's "open source is a red herring." The moat is post-training on your own data on an open runtime, and the cheap, high-leverage first rung is owning your embeddings, reused across search, fraud, recsys, and agent RAG. [[Hosting Is Table Stakes Extending Is the Moat]] [[Own Your Embeddings Before You Own Your Model]]
